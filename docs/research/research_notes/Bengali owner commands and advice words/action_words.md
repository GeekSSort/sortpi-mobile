## 1. What is the full verb set for recording payments, credit, sales, returns, stock in/out and edits, with inflected and misspelt forms?

### Takeaway
The real work is done by three light verbs, দেওয়া (deoya, give), নেওয়া (neoya, take) and করা (kora, do), plus পাওয়া (pawa, get), combined with a money/goods noun; direction lives in the verb's person ending (-lam = I/we, -lo = he/she/they) and in the postposition on the party, not in the noun. Cited sources confirm the paradigms of give/take and the dual meaning of "baki"; everything shop-specific (joma, shodh, bayna, memo, ferot, mal tola) is [MK] and must be validated with users.

### Cited Findings
- দেওয়া "to give": simple past দিলাম (dilam, I), দিলে (dile, you), দিল (dilo, he/she/they), দিলেন (dilen, polite); present perfect দিয়েছি (diyechi, I), দিয়েছ (diyecho, you), দিয়েছে (diyeche, he/she), দিয়েছেন (diyechen, polite); future দিব/দেব (dibo/debo), দিবে/দেবে (dibe/debe, you or he/she), দিবেন/দেবেন; "dao" is the familiar imperative (listed as the simple-present form). It also works as an auxiliary meaning "to finally do something / finish doing it". Antonym: নেওয়া. — [Wiktionary দেওয়া](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A6%A6%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%93%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE)
- নেওয়া "to take": simple past নিলাম (nilam), নিলে (nile), নিল (nilo), নিলেন (nilen); present perfect নিয়েছি (niyechi), নিয়েছ (niyecho), নিয়েছে (niyeche), নিয়েছেন (niyechen); future নিব/নেব (nibo/nebo); imperative-type forms নিস (nish, very familiar), নাও (nao, familiar), নেন (nen, polite). Example given: "ami tomar theke boitta nibo" = I'll take the book from you. — [Wiktionary নেওয়া](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A6%A8%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%93%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE)
- বাকি (baki) is both "remainder / the rest / extra" and "due or debt, money still owed"; example "ami bakir taka porishodh kore phelechi" = I have paid off the money I owed; "bakita amake dao" = give me the rest. Alternative spelling বাকী. — [Wiktionary বাকি](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A6%AC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%95%E0%A6%BF)
- ধার (dhar) noun senses include "debt or loan" (also edge, sharpness, stream); no usage with দেওয়া/নেওয়া shown. — [Wiktionary ধার](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A6%A7%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B0)
- Negation: না (na) after the finite verb ("kaj kori na"); -নি (-ni) negates perfect/past perfect ("korini"); নেই (nei) negates existential ("gari nei"); copula negation নই/নও/নন/নয় (noy). Imperative forms listed: familiar বলো (bolo), polite বলুন (bolun), 3rd person বলুক (boluk). — [Wikipedia Bengali grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_grammar)
- Competitor/market wording: TallyKhata's own blog writes "baki" in Roman letters ("if your customers take a lot of baki"), and describes tracking "how much they owe you and how much got repaid", with payable / receivable / cash-at-hand totals and a "Tagada Message" reminder — the page's visible text is English, so the Bangla labels পাবো/দেবো/জমা were NOT confirmed there. — [TallyKhata blog](https://www.tallykhata.com/7-reasons-why-tallykhata-is-the-perfect-bookkeeping-payment-solution-for-shops-and-small-businesses/)
- A Bangla-titled dues app is called "ব্যবসার হিসাব – বাকি খাতা" (romanised "Baki Khata"); its listing is English ("Record daily due amounts", "Record customer payments", "customer-wise balance details"). — [AppGoblin listing](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.bbz.bebsharhisab)
- Credit practice in TallyKhata coverage is one-directional (customers owe the shop; each credit purchase is entered, customer gets SMS of price and current balance); a shopkeeper quoted on a Tk2,913 month-end balance dispute. Bangla ledger terms were not used in the article. — [TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/feature/panorama/tallykhata-digitising-culture-buying-and-selling-credit-154465)
- (snippet only) Indian apps frame the same ledger as "udhar" (credit) and "jama" (payments received) — OkCredit search summary; West Bengal/India usage, not verified for Bangladesh. — [OkCredit](https://okcredit.in/bahi-khata-app)
- Bangladesh note: currency "taka" (BD) vs "rupees" (India); "kono bhangti nai" (I have no change) and "apnar kachhe bhangti ache?" show kachhe + nai as ordinary shop speech. — [Wikibooks Bengali/Shopping](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Bengali/Shopping)

**E. Question / report forms that look like commands but are not**
"koto baki?" (how much is due), "Karim er baki koto", "baki ache?" (is there due) are queries. "dekhi / dekho" = "let's see / look". Future (-bo) and "pore" (later) are not now. Tense/mood classification (command, past report, future promise, question, negated) must precede entity extraction.

**F. Spelling noise** (all [MK]): Banglish drops long vowels and aspirates (dhar/dar, ferot/pherot/ferat, shodh/sodh/sudh, mal/maal, taka/taka/tk/tka, nilam/nelam/nilaam, dilam/dielam/dilaam, hoyeche/hoise/hoice/hoyese, eseche/esheche/eshechhe/esechhe, joma/jama/zoma, bayna/baina/bainaa, nosto/noshto/nashto), double-consonant and "chh" spelling, "ph" for "f", "v" for "bh" (vul/bhul), "z" for "j". ## 2. How is the DIRECTION of money or goods expressed, and what are the most dangerous ambiguities (baki, dhar, pabo/dibo, dilam/nilam, ke/theke)?

### Takeaway
Direction is a computation from four inputs: (1) the giving/taking verb (দেওয়া vs নেওয়া vs পাওয়া), (2) the person ending (-lam I/we vs -lo he/she/they vs -len polite), (3) the postposition on the party (ke = recipient; theke / er kach theke = source), and (4) the party's role and existing balance (customer, supplier, staff, self). If any of the four is missing or conflicting, the assistant must ask, not guess.

### Cited Findings
- Give/take are exact antonyms and the first-person past endings differ only in the first consonant (দিলাম dilam / নিলাম nilam); third person past দিল (dilo) / নিল (nilo) differs from first person only by the ending -lam vs -lo; present perfect দিয়েছি/নিয়েছি (I) vs দিয়েছে/নিয়েছে (he/she). — [Wiktionary দেওয়া](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A6%A6%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%93%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE), [নেওয়া](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A6%A8%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%93%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE)
- "baki" means both "remainder" and "money still owed": the same word is used for "give me the rest" and "I paid off the debt". — [Wiktionary বাকি](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A6%AC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%95%E0%A6%BF)
- Case markers: genitive -র/-এর (-r/-er); objective -কে (-ke) in West Bengal and -রে (-re) common in Bangladesh; ablative থেকে (theke, "from"); postpositions follow the noun. Locative -তে/-এ/-য়; colloquial speech often uses -তে. — [Wikipedia Bengali grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_grammar)
- "apnar kachhe" (with you / in your possession) is the ordinary construction for possession: "apnar kachhe bhangti ache?" — [Wikibooks Bengali/Shopping](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Bengali/Shopping)
- Credit flows are modelled by TallyKhata as customers owing the shop, with SMS of price and balance sent to the customer — the app side is "receivable"-centric; supplier side is a separate "payable" total. — [TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/feature/panorama/tallykhata-digitising-culture-buying-and-selling-credit-154465), [TallyKhata blog](https://www.tallykhata.com/7-reasons-why-tallykhata-is-the-perfect-bookkeeping-payment-solution-for-shops-and-small-businesses/)
- Related fact (snippet only): a Daily Star feature described a customer SMS "Today's purchase 320 BDT, total due 1170 BDT". — [Daily Star supplement PDF](https://epfs.thedailystar.net/TDS/2024/05/30/Bangladesh/SUP/5_02/78bab4010af163b5_02.pdf)

### Inferences
**Direction grid for the speaker = shop owner/staff ("I"):** [MK]

| Surface pattern | Reads as | Money | Account effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| X ke N taka dilam | I gave X N | OUT | X customer: lent more (dhar) or refund / reduces customer advance; X supplier: reduces what I owe; X staff: advance/salary |
| X er kach theke N taka niyechi | I took N from X | IN | X customer: reduces what X owes me (or pays cash sale); X supplier: refund or I borrowed; X lender: I borrowed |
| X N taka dilo / diyeche | X gave me N | IN | same as above row 2 |
| X ke N taka dibo | I owe/will pay X | none now | future payable or reminder |
| X er kach theke N taka pabo | I will get N from X | none now | future receivable |
| X ke N taka dhar dilam | I lent X | OUT (if cash) or none (if goods) | X owes me more |
| X er kach theke N taka dhar nilam | I borrowed | IN | I owe X |
| X er baki N | X's due is N | none | usually X owes me; if X is a supplier, I owe X |

**Most dangerous ambiguities (rank by loss if wrong):**
1. dilam vs dilo vs dilen (and nilam vs nilo vs nilen). One vowel in Banglish ("dilam"/"dilo"), and STT often normalises the ending. Flipped result: money IN becomes OUT, balances move the wrong way. Always show the interpretation in words with an arrow, never only the number.
2. Subject drop (pro-drop). Bangla commonly omits "I/he": "500 taka dilo" has no subject; "Karim 500 dilo" has subject Karim; "500 dilo Karim ke" would mean someone gave to Karim (subject dropped, ke = recipient). The two Karim readings are opposite.
3. ke vs theke vs er kach(e) theke. "Karim ke ... nilam" (I took Karim?) is odd; if heard, it is probably "Karim er kach theke" with theke lost. "ke" is also the question word কে (who): "ke dilo?" (who gave?). In Banglish both are "ke". theke also shows value-origin ("120 theke 100 koro"), not a party.
4. baki (remainder vs due vs change) and baki ferot. "Baki 400 dao" may be "give me the remaining 400 (change)" or "the 400 still owed is to be paid". Total-minus-paid checksum (see Q4/Q5).
5. dhar vs baki vs joma. A customer taking goods on credit is "baki" (customer owes), but lending cash is "dhar dilam"; "joma" is payment in the customer's ledger. Same-sentence mixes ("Rahim er 500 baki joma koro") are possible; two entries or one? Ask.
6. pabo vs dibo (future tense) vs pelam/dilam (past). Future = a promise/reminder, not a ledger movement. Many users still say "500 pabo" meaning "he owes me 500" (a balance statement, not a payment). Treat as "set/adjust baki" only after explicit confirmation of which.
7. ke is ALSO used for who receives the bill: "Karim ke bill dao" = give Karim a bill (a sale), not a payment.
8. diye: "bkash diye dilo" = by bKash (channel); "Karim diye pathalam" = through Karim (intermediary). A name before diye is not the payee.
9. Third party chains: "Rahim er hoye Karim dilo" (Karim paid on behalf of Rahim). Rare; reject and ask.
10. joma = deposit at the bank = cash leaves the till but is not an expense to the customer ledger.

**Bangladesh vs West Bengal:** objective -re in Bangladesh (Wikipedia grammar, cited above): "Karimre 500 taka dilam" may appear in spoken/Banglish Dhaka style; STT/tokenizer must treat -re and -ke alike and strip suffix when matching names. "udhar" (India) vs "dhar/baki" (Bangladesh) (uncertain). Currency taka vs rupee (cited). Otherwise the give/take verbs and "baki" are the same.

### Gaps
- No source states which party in a TallyKhata-style entry is "got" vs "gave" in Bangla; Bangla UI strings were not seen.
- The pro-drop and word-order claims are [MK]; the Wikipedia page did not state SOV explicitly and gave only a few SOV examples.
- The behaviour of Bangla speech recognition on verb endings (-lam/-lo) is untested; no source.

## 3. How are amounts, units and parties placed in a sentence, and what parsing rules follow (Bangla digits, "dui hajar", "deRh", "sare tin")?

### Takeaway
Typical order is Party(+case) – amount(+currency/unit) – verb (SOV), with free movement and afterthought repetition, and numbers appear as Bangla digits, Bangla words, Latin digits, or Banglish words. A parser should find the verb, bind each postposition to its noun, then read the quantity chunk. Several number words collide with ordinary words (নয় = nine AND "is not"), so numbers need a range check and a read-back in both digits and words.

### Cited Findings
- Bangla digits and number words: ০ শূন্য, ১ এক, ২ দুই, ৩ তিন, ৪ চার, ৫ পাঁচ, ৬ ছয়, ৭ সাত, ৮ আট, ৯ নয়; 10–19 irregular (দশ, এগারো, বারো, তেরো, চৌদ্দ, পনেরো, ষোলো, সতেরো, আঠারো, ঊনিশ); tens বিশ/কুড়ি, ত্রিশ/তিরিশ, চল্লিশ, পঞ্চাশ, ষাট, সত্তর, আশি, নব্বই; 100 = শত/একশ, 1,000 হাজার, 10,000 দশ হাজার/অযুত, 100,000 লক্ষ/লাখ, 10 million কোটি. — [Wikipedia Bengali numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_numerals)
- Fraction prefixes: সাড়ে (sare, "and a half"), সোয়া (soya, +1/4), পৌনে (poune, -1/4); আধুলি (half), সিকি/পোয়া (quarter). — [Wikipedia Bengali numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_numerals) (the article did NOT cover দেড়, আড়াই or ডজন)
- Classifiers: -টা (-ta) generic, -টি (-ti) formal, -জন (-jon) humans only. — [Wikipedia Bengali grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_grammar)
- Shop quantity words: সের (sher, about 0.933 kg), পোয়া (poa, quarter sher), কেজি, গ্রাম, লিটার; হালি (hali = 4), ডজন (dojon = 12), হাফ ডজন; আধা কেজি (half kg); "aro ekta" (one more), "ekta kom" (one less); "kono bhangti nai"; "ফাইভ হান্ড্রেড (pãchsho) takar bhangti". — [Wikibooks Bengali/Shopping](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Bengali/Shopping)
- Question forms: "dam kôto?", "kôto taka?", "kôto lagbe?" — [Wikibooks Bengali/Shopping](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Bengali/Shopping)
- Postpositions follow their noun; genitive/objective/ablative markers as above. — [Wikipedia Bengali grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_grammar)
- (snippet only) Academic Bangla spoken-numeral recognition work covers digits 0–9 and 0–99 (CNN around 89.6% on 0–99 in one dataset); no standard Bangla speech corpus; compound large numbers like "hajar/shoto" were not covered. — [BanglaNum, arXiv 2403.13465](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13465v1)

### Inferences
**Common sentence frames** [MK], ordered by how often I expect them:
1. `[Party]-ke [amount] taka dilam` — Karim ke 500 taka dilam.
2. `[Party]-er kach theke [amount] niyechi` — Rahim er kach theke 2000 niyechi.
3. `[Party] [amount] taka dilo/diyeche` — Karim 500 taka dilo.
4. `[Amount] taka [Party]-ke dilam` — free reorder.
5. Afterthought: `dilam Karim ke 500` or `500 niyechi, Rahim er kach theke`.
6. Item+qty: `[Party]-ke [item] [qty] [unit] [price] taka (dam) bikri korlam` — "Rahim ke chini 2 kg 240 taka".
7. Total / paid / due: `mot 1000, 600 dilo, baki 400` (here baki = remainder = due).
8. Verb-only commands: `bill koro`, `baki likho` with party/amount earlier or later.
9. Bare noun phrase with no verb: `Karim 500` (direction unknown; ask).

**Parsing rules:**
- R1. Find the verb (usually last, sometimes first), read mood (-o/-un command, -lam/-lo past, -yechi/-yeche perfect, -bo future, -na/-ni/-noy negation).
- R2. Strip suffixes from names (-ke, -er, -r, -re, -ere) and match the stem; "Karimer" = "Karim er". Also handle spaced and fused forms (kach theke / kacheta theke / kachhe theke).
- R3. Postposition binding: ke = recipient/object; er kach theke / theke = source; diye = channel or intermediary; e / te = location or channel ("bkash e"); er = "of" (Rahim er baki = Rahim's due).
- R4. Number chunk = (digits or words) + optional multiplier (shoto, hajar, lakh) + optional currency (taka/tk/৳/টাকা) + optional unit/classifier. Currency appears AFTER number in Bangla ("500 taka") but Latin-style "tk 500" appears in typed text.
- R5. Accept both digit systems: Latin 0–9, Bangla ০–৯, and mixed ("৫00"); convert Bangla digits first.
- R6. Number words to value [MK]: "dui hajar" 2000; "dui hajar pach sho" 2500; "sare tin hajar" 3500; "sare tin sho" 350; "deRh hajar" (দেড় হাজার) 1500; "aRai hajar" (আড়াই হাজার) 2500; "paune dui hajar" 1750; "soya sho" 125; "ek lakh" 100000; "dash hajar" 10000; "pachsho" 500; "panch sho" 500; "tin sho" 300; "tirish" 30. Pattern: sare/soya/paune/deRh/aRai modify the following unit, not the preceding. dozen/hali (12/4) are unit multipliers: "ek dojon" = 12, "adha dojon" = 6.
- R7. Collisions to validate: নয় (9) vs নয় (is not): "noy taka" vs "ei ta noy"; পাঁচ (5) vs পাঁচশ (500) vs পঞ্চাশ (50) vs পনেরো (15); ত্রিশ (30) vs তিনশ (300); সাত (7) vs সাতাশ (27) vs সাতশ (700); দুই (2) vs দুই শ vs দুই হাজার; "ek" can be 1 or the article "a". STT drops "sho/hajar"; always compare to the customer's usual range and to the stated total.
- R8. Latin shorthand: "2k" = 2000, "5/-", "500/=" , "tk", "tka", "৳". Acceptable but read back in words.
- R9. Units: ta/ti/khana/pis/piece/pcs/jon/dojon/hali/kg/gm/ser/pouya/litre/packet/carton/bosta/joda; ask when qty is given without unit and item has more than one unit.
- R10. Checksum for baki sentences: total − paid = baki. If not equal, do not save; ask which number is wrong.
- R11. Absolute vs delta for master data: "dam 20 komao" = reduce by 20; "dam 20 koro / 20 e dao" = set to 20; "120 theke 100 koro" = change 120 to 100 (theke here = from-value, not a party). Same for stock: "5 barao" (+5) vs "stock 5 koro" (=5).
- R12. Date words: "kal" = yesterday OR tomorrow, "porshu" = day after tomorrow OR day before yesterday; disambiguate with tense (kal dilam = yesterday; kal dibo = tomorrow).
- R13. Multiple numbers in one sentence (amount, qty, price): require a card to label each.

**Voice-to-text risk list** [MK]: names split or merged ("Abdul Karim" vs "Karim"), same-sound names (Rahim/Rohim, Karim/Korim, Salam/Salim), nicknames and shop names (Mia bhai, Rashed vai, "dokaner Karim"), titles (bhai, apa, khala, chacha, mama, uncle), Bangla script vs Banglish mismatch with stored names; code-mixed numerals ("five hundred taka", "পাঁচ hundred"); misheard dilam/dilo; dropped sho/hajar; "taka" mistaken for other words; duplicate-name customers (two Karims). Match names to saved parties by stem, show candidates with a distinguishing hint (area, last transaction), never auto-pick when two score close.

## 4. Which phrases cancel or correct a previous action, and how do people confirm?

### Takeaway
Negation attaches to the verb (na, ni, noy, nei/nai), but the same particle "na" doubles as a coaxing softener and tag question, and "thik ache"/"hobe"/"hu" can mean agreement or just "I heard you". For money actions the safe pattern is to require a tap on an explicit button; voice "yes" can only act as a shortcut for the exact card shown, and a correction must always be applied to the pending card, not to the saved record.

### Cited Findings
- না follows the finite verb ("kaj kori na"); -নি negates perfect/past perfect ("korini"); নেই negates existence; copula negation নই/নও/নন/নয়. — [Wikipedia Bengali grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_grammar)
- "nai" appears as an ordinary colloquial negation ("kono bhangti nai"). — [Wikibooks Bengali/Shopping](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Bengali/Shopping)
- দেওয়া also works as an auxiliary meaning "to finish doing it", so "kore dao" combines doing with completion. — [Wiktionary দেওয়া](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A6%A6%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%93%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE)
- Imperative forms by politeness: boló (familiar), bolun (polite). — [Wikipedia Bengali grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_grammar)
- The disputed-balance story underlines why a visible, correctable record matters: customers told TBS they feel shop owners write more than actual; SMS of price and balance reduced disputes. — [TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/feature/panorama/tallykhata-digitising-culture-buying-and-selling-credit-154465)

### Inferences

**Confirmation words** [MK]
Positive: হ্যাঁ / হ্যা / হ্যান / হু / হুম (hya, hyan, han, ha, hu, hum), ঠিক আছে / ঠিকাছে (thik ache, thikache, thik ase), ওকে / অকে / ওকে (ok, okay, oke), করো / করে দাও / করে ফেলো (koro, kore dao, kore felo), হবে (hobe), আছে (ache), জি / জী (ji), সেভ করো (save koro).
Risk: "thik ache" is often a reaction ("fine, I see") rather than consent to save; "hobe" = "that will do" or "it will happen"; "hu/hum" can be a filler. Short Banglish "ha" and "na" are one letter apart on a phone keyboard. A spoken "na" after a negative question is ambiguous in Bangla (it can confirm the negative or deny the question) (uncertain).

**Design rules from this** [MK]
- Never ask a negative question ("save na korbo?"); show the card with two named buttons: "Save (সেভ করুন)" and "Change (বদলান)" plus "Cancel (বাতিল)".
- Voice confirmation: accept only a whitelist (hya / ha / thik ache / ok / koro / kore dao / save koro) AND only when the card is on screen and the same card was read back; any other utterance changes or cancels the card. Treat "na", "noy", "thik na", "bhul", "thak", "bad dao", "ageta" as a stop signal.
- Corrections apply to the pending card first ("500 na, 5000"): replace amount and re-show. For an already-saved record show the saved entry with Edit / Undo, and require a new card for the reversal (it posts a counter-entry; never silently delete).
- "Cancel the last one" must show which one (party, amount, time) before executing.
- Delay finalisation by a short window and show a visible Undo for about 10 to 30 seconds after save (user memory file says status/undo/next step/retry are required).

- How Bangladeshi speakers answer negative questions with na/hya in practice was not found.
- "ulte dao" as an idiom for undo was not verified (uncertain).

## 5. What confirm-before-act design is appropriate (what must be shown back), including risk handling, voice-to-text mistakes, duplicates and idempotency?

### Takeaway
Every sentence that writes or moves money must produce a confirmation card showing party, amount (digits and words), direction (arrow + colour + plain Bangla sentence), the effect on that party's balance (before and after), the date, the account/channel, and the user's original words; saving must be idempotent per card. This list of cited sources contains no design standard; the card design below is reasoning from the language findings above plus the project's own colour and recovery rules.

### Cited Findings
- Real shopkeepers dispute balances and fear overwritten entries; instant SMS of price and total due reduced disputes for one retailer (Tk2,913 month-end balance). — [TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/feature/panorama/tallykhata-digitising-culture-buying-and-selling-credit-154465)
- Competitor apps already show payable / receivable / cash-at-hand totals and balance per customer, which are the numbers a card should echo. — [TallyKhata blog](https://www.tallykhata.com/7-reasons-why-tallykhata-is-the-perfect-bookkeeping-payment-solution-for-shops-and-small-businesses/), [AppGoblin listing](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.bbz.bebsharhisab)
- (snippet only) Business Post describes halkhata as a notebook record system maintained by small shopkeepers; one research diary says poor baki management is a common cause of shop failure and "you can't run a shop without giving baki". — [Business Post](https://businesspostbd.com/opinion-todays-paper/lost-tradition-of-halkhata-2023-04-14)

### Inferences
**Risk tiers** [MK reasoning]

| Tier | Actions | Required handling |
|---|---|---|
| A: money or balance changes | payment in/out, joma, dhar dilam/nilam, baki likho, shodh, advance/bayna, supplier payment, refund (ferot), bill with credit, waive/chhar, any cancel/delete/undo of a money record | Always the full confirmation card; no voice-only commit; counter-entry for reversal |
| B: stock changes | mal eseche, mal gelo, damage, stock count (SET), exchange | Full card (stock count and damage especially); show item, qty, unit, before to after |
| C: master data | dam bodlao (delta vs set!), naam bodlao, add item/customer | Card with old to new; price changes show the delta explicitly |
| D: read-only | koto baki, stock koto, hishab dekhao | No card |

Recommendation: every write goes through a card in v1. Reduce friction only after measuring; skip-the-card (with Undo) is a product decision for the owner to make, not a default.

**Card contents (the "show it back" set)**
1. Verb in plain Bangla past tense with the subject made explicit, and the original words quoted: "আপনি করিমকে ৫০০ টাকা দিয়েছেন" (you gave Karim 500 taka) vs "করিম আপনাকে ৫০০ টাকা দিয়েছে" (Karim gave you 500 taka). Always include the subject.
2. Party chip with role (customer / supplier / staff) and a disambiguator when more than one candidate (phone ending, last transaction).
3. Amount in big digits AND words (৫০০ - পাঁচশো টাকা); thousand separators in Bangladeshi lakh style (1,00,000).
4. Direction arrow + words ("টাকা ঢুকলো / টাকা বের হলো") with the project colour code (green money-in, red owed/finished, blue go, icon beside colour so colour is not the only cue — user memory).
5. Effect line: "করিমের কাছে পাওনা: ১২০০ → ১৭০০" (balance before to after, with who owes whom spelled out). If after crosses zero (customer becomes owed money), show a warning.
6. Date and channel (cash / bKash / Nagad); default today, show the word "আজ".
7. For bills and returns: line items with qty+unit+price; for baki sentences show total, paid, baki and verify the equation.
8. Buttons: Save, Change (opens pending fields), Cancel. Optional "wrong person?" to swap party.

**When to ask a question instead of showing a card** (hard stops)
- Missing party, missing amount, missing direction ("Karim 500"), or verb only ("joma koro").
- Two parties or two amounts in one sentence.
- Party role conflicts with the verb (e.g. "supplier ke dhar dilam").
- Amount outside the party's normal range (e.g. ≥ 10x median) or a number whose reading collides (noy/nine, tirish/tinsho).
- Total−paid≠baki.
- Past/future mismatch (dibo/pabo are future: offer "set reminder" or "record as due", not payment).
- Command contains a negation or hedge (na, noy, thak, pore, dekhi) or is a question.
- Sentence is a correction to an earlier saved record.

**Duplicates and idempotency** [MK]
- Generate a client-side idempotency key when the card is created; save endpoint accepts it once (a double-tap or retry returns the first result).
- Content fingerprint (party + amount + direction + kind + date, within a few minutes). If seen, show "এটা আগেই সেভ হয়েছে (৩ মিনিট আগে) - আবার সেভ করবেন?"; do not silently drop legitimate repeats (two 500s from the same customer happen).
- "abar" or a repeated sentence in the voice buffer is a duplicate candidate, not a new transaction.
- Offline/queued saves keep the same key; show "পাঠানো বাকি" state until acknowledged; on retry, check the key first.
- Reversals are new linked entries ("Undo of X"); do not delete rows, and do not apply an Undo to anything but the exact record shown.

**Voice-to-text safeguards**
- Read the amount back in words when the source is voice; for amounts ≥ 1,000 require a second look (tap on the figure to edit).
- Highlight the verb ending ("দিলাম" in bold) when the sentence includes dilam/dilo/nilam/nilo so the user sees the direction cue the system used.
- Keep original audio text for audit; show STT confidence only as a hint, never as a gate.
- Prefer showing two parsed candidates when the model is torn ("আপনি দিলেন / করিম দিলো?") rather than one guess.

### Gaps
- No source on confirmation design in Bangladeshi bookkeeping apps; I could not see TallyKhata's actual confirmation screens. All design above is reasoning.
- No evidence from Bangladeshi users on whether they prefer "Save" or "ঠিক আছে" wording; test with real users.
- No data on how often shopkeepers say a sentence twice in speech input; idempotency window sizes are guesses.
- Not covered: bKash "Cash Out/Send Money" Bangla labels (search returned fee news only); the card should not copy MFS wording until verified.
