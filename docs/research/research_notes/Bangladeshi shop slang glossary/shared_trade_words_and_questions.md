# Cross-trade Bangla / Banglish vocabulary and phone-question phrasing for Bangladeshi shopkeepers

Method note. Web research confirmed the datasets, the numerals, the calendar facts, the "kal" dictionary senses, the traditional counting units and the app wording cited below. I found no corpus of real shopkeeper questions. Everything under "Inferences" that is a word list or phrasing table comes from general knowledge of Bangla and Bangladeshi retail speech. It is NOT web-verified. Where I am less sure, it is marked (uncertain). "BD" means Bangladesh, "WB" means West Bengal / India. Forms are written Banglish / Bangla script.

---

## 1. Which words and spellings are most common for each concept, and what are the typical misspellings? (core question words and data vocabulary)

### Takeaway
Shop talk is a small closed set of question words plus 15 to 20 data nouns, each with 3 to 8 spellings. The spellings vary because Bangla letters map many-to-one onto Latin letters (see section 5). Practical vocabulary can be enumerated. Spelling must be handled by normalization, not by listing every variant.

### Cited Findings
- Bangla-language khata apps for BD shops use "baki" (money customers owe on credit) and "khata" (খাতা, ledger) as the core words. TallyKhata also names its reminder feature "Tagada Message" and mixes English terms (cash at hand, due collection, sales, purchases, expenses, customers and suppliers) with Bangla script product names — [TallyKhata](https://www.tallykhata.com/7-reasons-why-tallykhata-is-the-perfect-bookkeeping-payment-solution-for-shops-and-small-businesses/)
- Ledgeo (BD khata/POS app, Bangla and English) has a "Baki Khata" module: who owes, what is overdue, who paid in advance, credit limits per customer — [Search summary of Ledgeo Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ledgeo.app) (vendor marketing; the page text itself could not be fetched)
- Hishabee markets a digital khata (customer names/numbers, low-stock alerts, SMS reminders for unpaid dues) — [Hishabee](https://blog.hishabee.io/?p=327) (vendor claim)
- Hisabun is a bilingual Bengali-English SME app with business khata, cashbook, stock alerts, loan/debt management — [Trustpilot listing](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.hisabun.com)
- Informal romanization has no standard. Wikipedia lists several systems (ISO 15919, ITRANS, Harvard-Kyoto, Avro) and notes known mismatches: অ is written "a" in ISO but pronounced /ɔ~o/ and written "o" in Avro; ভ can be "bh" or "v"; ফ can be "ph" or "f"; শ/ষ/স are all usually pronounced [ʃ]; homophones such as শাপ / সাপ are identical in sound — [Romanisation of Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanisation_of_Bengali)
- A sociolinguistic study of Romanized Bangla-English code-switching drew on Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp posts from BD — [IJELR](https://jurnal.utu.ac.id/IJELR/article/view/14906)
- The term "kal" has the senses tomorrow, yesterday, time/era, season, grammatical tense — [Wiktionary কাল](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/কাল)

## 2. How is 'kal' (yesterday/tomorrow) resolved, and how are relative dates expressed?

### Takeaway
Wiktionary confirms কাল means both yesterday and tomorrow (plus time, season, tense). The verb tense in the question decides the direction; in a data assistant, "past tense or past-participle result form" means yesterday and anything else means tomorrow or is unanswerable. Bangladeshi calendar facts are fixed and cited below.

### Cited Findings
- কাল means "tomorrow" (synonyms আগামীকাল, কালকে) AND "yesterday" (synonyms গতকাল, কালকে); also time/era, season (e.g. বর্ষাকাল), and grammatical tense (বর্তমান কাল = present tense) — [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/কাল)
- The Bangladeshi national calendar was reformed by a committee under Muhammad Shahidullah (Bangla Academy, 1966) and adopted officially in 1987; since then 1 Baishakh (Pohela Boishakh) is always 14 April in BD, while in WB it varies between 14 and 15 April — [Bangladeshi national calendar, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_national_calendar); [BanglaDateToday](https://www.bangladatetoday.com/blog/pohela-boishakh-1434-date-and-why-april-14)
- Sources conflict on month lengths: one account has Boishakh to Bhadra at 31 days, Ashwin to Magh at 30, Falgun 29/30; another says a 2019 revision made Boishakh to Ashwin 31 days, Kartik to Magh 30, Falgun 29 (30 in leap years), Chaitra 30. A 1996 update is also mentioned. Not resolved — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_national_calendar); [BanglaDateToday months](https://www.bangladatetoday.com/blog/bengali-months-names-meaning)

#### 2b. Disambiguating "kal" and "porshu" (rule set to implement)
1. Look at the verb or complement in the same utterance. Past markers: -echhe/-eche (hoyeche, korechhi), -echilo/-echhilo (hoyechilo), -lo (holo, gelo, dilo), -lam (dilam), "ashlo", "bikri holo", "chilo", "ferot ashlo". If present → **kal = yesterday**.
2. Future markers: -be (hobe, korbo, dibo, anbo, ashbe), "lagbe", "dite hobe", "ashbo", "kinbo", "jabe" → **kal = tomorrow**.
3. No verb ("kal er bikri", "kal er bill gulo"): for an analytics assistant default to **yesterday**, because data exists only for the past; optionally ask when the noun is a plan/order ("kal er order", "kal er delivery", "kal er due" = due tomorrow? uncertain).
4. "kal ... diye dibo / kal pabo / kal dibo" (promise to pay) = tomorrow; customer-promise phrases always future.
5. Compound "kal raate": past tense → last night, future → tomorrow night.
6. Same rule for "porshu": past tense → day before yesterday; future → day after tomorrow. "Gotokal", "porshu er ager din" are unambiguous alternatives.
7. Disambiguation also works through qualifiers: "gotokal", "ager din", "agamikal", "pore", "ei kal" (kal in compound = era: "ekhonkar kal" = nowadays: ignore).
8. Spelling forms "kalke", "kaleke", "kalka" are all the same word + -ke/-ke locative; "kal-ke" gets the same rule. "kal" may also be কাল "black" only as কালো (kalo); rarely in shops (color filter in fashion/fabric: "kalo shirt"). Note kal vs kalo.
9. Typical LLM prompt rule: "Resolve 'kal/kalke/kaleke/kal er' to yesterday if the sentence uses past tense (-lo, -echhe, -echilo, ferot ashlo) or has no verb; tomorrow if -be/-bo/lagbe/hobe; ask if still ambiguous."

#### 2c. Calendars and date formats
- Two calendars in parallel: Gregorian for trade, banking, bKash statements, invoices; Bengali (Bangabda) mostly for Pohela Boishakh, haal-khata, and some festival timing. Shop owners rarely ask "Ashar mashe koto bikri?" in daily use; "Boishakh mash" is likely only around halkhata (হালখাতা: the traditional new-year ledger/closing of old accounts, important for baki settlement; sweets shops, jewellers, fabric traders). (uncertain frequency).
- Bengali months: Boishakh, Joishtho (Jyoishtho), Asharh (Ashar), Shrabon (Sraban), Bhadro (Bhadra), Ashwin (Ashshin), Kartik, Ogrohayon (Agrahayan), Poush, Magh, Falgun, Choitro (Chaitra). Bangla: বৈশাখ, জ্যৈষ্ঠ, আষাঢ়, শ্রাবণ, ভাদ্র, আশ্বিন, কার্তিক, অগ্রহায়ণ, পৌষ, মাঘ, ফাল্গুন, চৈত্র. BD Pohela Boishakh is fixed at 14 April (cited). WB differs (14 or 15). Do NOT accept Bengali-month dates without a mapping table; recommended: map to Gregorian via the BD scheme.
- Gregorian month names are used in English letters or Bangla transliteration: January → জানুয়ারি, February → ফেব্রুয়ারি, March → মার্চ, April → এপ্রিল, May → মে, June → জুন, July → জুলাই, August → আগস্ট, September → সেপ্টেম্বর, October → অক্টোবর, November → নভেম্বর, December → ডিসেম্বর.
- Date formats: day/month/year is standard (25/12/2026, ২৫/১২/২০২৬, 25-12-26, 25 Dec). Month-first (12/25) is unusual; treat dd/mm as default. Ordinal day suffixes in Bangla script: ১লা, ২রা, ৩রা, ৪ঠা, ৫ই ... ১০ই, ১১ই..., ১৯শে, ২০শে ... ৩০শে, ৩১শে. "tarikh" = date.
- Weekday base: week conventionally starts Saturday in BD; Friday is the weekly holiday (many shops open late Friday or are shut; markets differ by area). "ei soptahe" can mean calendar week starting Saturday or "last 7 days" — implement as rolling 7 days or ask. (uncertain)
- Religious / seasonal anchors: Eid-ul-Fitr, Eid-ul-Adha (Qurbani Eid), Ramadan/Ramzan, Shab-e-Barat, Durga Puja, Pohela Boishakh, Pohela Falgun/Valentine's, Boi Mela (Feb, books), 21 Feb, 16 Dec, Winter/Shit (sweater, quilt, jackets), Barsha/Borsha (monsoon). "Eid er age" = in the weeks before Eid. Eid follows the lunar Hijri calendar and moves ~10 to 11 days earlier each Gregorian year, so "Eid er age" must be resolved from a holiday table per year, not a fixed date. (general knowledge, not verified in this session.)

### Gaps
- Official BD Bangla Academy month-length rule (1987 vs 2019 revision) unresolved; use a verified source before computing Bengali-month ranges.

## 3. What number/money expressions need parsing (lakh, hajar, Bangla digits, etc.)?

### Takeaway
Bangla digits ০–৯ and the South Asian grouping (lakh, crore) are standard in BD. ## 5. What conventions do Bangladeshis follow when typing Bangla in English letters, and where do they diverge? (Avro, Google Input Tools, SMS/chat conventions, voice-to-text)

### Takeaway
There is no standard. Avro Phonetic (case-sensitive: "T/D/N", "oo/u", "o" for অ) is the most common keyboard behind typed Bangla script, but Banglish messages are written ad hoc, mostly lower-case, dropping vowels and doubling letters freely. Matching needs a phonetic skeleton, not exact spellings.

### Cited Findings
- Avro vowels (third-party Borno docs): o → অ, a → আ, i → ই, I or ee → ঈ, u or oo → উ, U → ঊ — [Borno docs: Avro Phonetic Layout](https://borno.codepotro.com/docs/guide/layout-avro.html)
- OmicronLab says various JavaScript re-implementations of Avro Phonetic exist but none is 100% compatible — [OmicronLab developers](https://omicronlab.com/developers.html); Avro Keyboard overview — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro_Keyboard); official guide PDF "Bangla Typing with Avro Phonetic" — [OmicronLab PDF](https://www.omicronlab.com/download/pdf/Bangla%20Typing%20with%20Fixed%20Keyboard%20Layouts.pdf)
- Romanization ambiguities (o/a; bh/v; ph/f; sh/s/ch; case-sensitive retroflex letters) — [Romanisation of Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanisation_of_Bengali)
- Romanized Assamese social-media text shows one word with three different transliterations, and "transliteration or romanization guidelines are not established for most Indic languages" — [LREC-COLING 2024 Assamese paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.143.pdf) (analogous language, via search snippet)

(Engineering inference; no source verified.) #### 5c. Input method habits
- Avro Phonetic (Windows/Android "Avro Keyboard", iAvro on macOS) and Google Indic Keyboard / Gboard "Bangla (phonetic)" or "Bangla (transliteration)" produce Bangla-script text from Latin typing. - Android users often switch between Bangla script and Banglish within one chat. Facebook/Messenger/WhatsApp are the main contexts, so Banglish is the more likely typing mode for an assistant message box.
- Mixed within a sentence: "aj er bikri ta ক্যাশ ও bkash alada dekhao" — both scripts in one query; the matcher must tokenise per script and normalise both to one skeleton.

- Google Input Tools has no official transliteration rule document that I found.
- No measurement of the relative share of Bangla-script vs Banglish vs mixed typing among BD shopkeepers.

## 7. What research or open datasets exist (romanized/code-mixed Bangla, transliteration, Avro mappings) that could help build a matcher without hand-written question lists?

### Takeaway
Yes. Use Dakshina's Bengali lexicon (multi-annotator romanization variants with counts, CC BY-SA 4.0) as a variant dictionary, Aksharantar/IndicXlit and BanglaTLit/BanglaDual for back-transliteration models, and code-mixed Banglish social-media datasets for noisy register. Avro rules give a deterministic fallback. None of them are retail-domain, so a small domain lexicon (section 1c) must be added.

### Cited Findings
- **Dakshina (Google Research, LREC 2020, Roark et al.)**: native-script and Latin text for 12 South Asian languages incl. Bengali. Bengali has Wikipedia native text, a romanization lexicon (25,000 training word types, 2,500 dev, 2,500 test; each line is word, romanization, attestation count; most words have romanizations from multiple annotators, so variant spellings are included), and manually romanized full sentences with native-script pairs and validation files. Licence CC BY-SA 4.0. Release v1.0, 27 May 2020 — [GitHub](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/dakshina); [Paper (arXiv 2007.01176)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01176); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.294/); [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/pub49147/)
- **Aksharantar / IndicXlit (arXiv 2205.03018; EMNLP 2023 Findings)**: about 26 million transliteration pairs, 21 Indic languages, 12 scripts; mined from corpora plus human annotators; IndicXlit model is a multilingual transformer for romanized→native conversion, reported +15% over prior best on the Dakshina test set; code and models MIT, benchmark/manual data CC-BY. A table extraction showed roughly 1.34M Bengali training pairs (verify in paper) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03018)
- **BanglaTLit (EMNLP 2024 Findings)**: 42.7k back-transliteration samples of romanized Bangla plus a 245.7k BanglaTLit-PT pre-training corpus; baselines; code and data on GitHub (farhanishmam/BanglaTLit) — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.859)
- **BanglaDual (Mendeley Data, 2026, CC BY 4.0)**: 1,122,189 Banglish→Bangla transliteration pairs and 119,727 Banglish→English translation pairs, merged from six public sources (including nibir1234/banglish_to_bengali, BanglaTLit, BanTH, sk-community/romanized_bangla); 85% Wikipedia text, 7% emotion, 3% hate speech; spelling variation not discussed — [Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/pxw2bb4wyt/1)
- **Code-mixed Chaos (Mendeley, 2025)**: 10,234 Banglish social-media comments, toxicity labels, with a Bangla-script version — [Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/23dp3t88vk/1)
- **MixSarc (2026)**: 9,087 Bangla-English sentences (humor, sarcasm, offensive, vulgarity) on Hugging Face; lists BanglishRev (1.74M product-review entries) and BanTH (37,300 hate-speech entries) as related — [arXiv 2602.21608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21608)
- **BnSentMix (2024)**: 20,000 samples from Facebook, YouTube and e-commerce, 4 sentiment labels; combined version on Hugging Face — [HF dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/asfaqur/bn_code_mix_sentiment_dataset)
- **Modeling Romanized Hindi and Bengali (arXiv 2511.22769)**: about 1.8M Hindi and 1M Bengali transliteration pairs, rule-based Bengali→Roman generation from Wikipedia, Marian seq2seq model; release status not stated; says existing resources lack spelling diversity — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22769)
- **Avro Phonetic**: pronunciation-based; reference docs at OmicronLab; third-party JS clones are not fully compatible — [OmicronLab developers](https://omicronlab.com/developers.html); [Wikipedia Avro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro_Keyboard)
- A BD Facebook/Instagram/WhatsApp Romanized-Bangla study exists as a qualitative reference — [IJELR](https://jurnal.utu.ac.id/IJELR/article/view/14906)

### Inferences
- A practical no-hand-written-questions pipeline (engineering suggestion, not from a source): (1) detect script; (2) normalise Bangla digits and diacritic variants; (3) convert Banglish to a phonetic skeleton (collapse o/a, i/ee/y, u/oo, s/sh, j/z, b/v/bh, ph/f, w/o, repeated letters, drop h after consonants); (4) fuzzy-match tokens to a domain lexicon (section 1c) built from Dakshina variants; (5) classify intent + extract slots (metric, entity, period, op) with embeddings or a small LLM; (6) resolve "kal/porshu" with tense rules (section 2b); (7) parse numbers/units (sections 3 and 4).
- Dakshina's attested-variant counts are the best public proxy for how often each spelling is used; Aksharantar/IndicXlit can be used to back-transliterate Banglish into Bangla for matching against Bangla-script lexicon.
- Datasets above are mostly Wikipedia, social media, hate-speech or sentiment text, so none cover shop vocabulary (baki, mojud, khoroch, memo); coverage of those words must be checked with a spot test before relying on them.

### Gaps
- Licences of BanglaTLit, BanTH, MixSarc, BnSentMix, Code-mixed Chaos were not checked; only Dakshina (CC BY-SA 4.0), BanglaDual (CC BY 4.0) and Aksharantar (MIT/CC-BY) were read.
- Not fetched or read in full: Dakshina Bengali file names, Aksharantar Bengali counts (verify), OmicronLab Avro PDF, Mahboob & Rahman 2017, Kirov et al. 2023.
- No dataset of Bengali speech-to-text errors for retail vocabulary found.
- Not covered: Google Input Tools rule documentation, Bangla Academy transliteration rules (Wikipedia page on the Bengali romanisation only), Facebook-commerce post corpora, Reddit r/bangladesh sampling.
