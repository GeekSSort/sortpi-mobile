# Bangladeshi shop slang glossary: Electronics and mobile, Auto parts, Hardware and paint

READ THIS FIRST (source quality). Web search (US-only tool) returned almost no source that documents spoken shop slang. Retailer sites (Star Tech, Bikroy, Othoba), Prothom Alo, and the BTRC/NEIR news confirm only the written/commercial forms of some terms. Most of the glossary below is therefore labelled "domain knowledge, no web source" = compiled from general knowledge of Bangladeshi retail speech, NOT verified against a citable page. Anything I am not confident about is also marked (uncertain). Treat every unsourced term as a candidate for validation with 3 to 5 real shopkeepers (Bangshal, Elephant Road/Multiplan, Nawabpur, Jatrabari, Chawkbazar) before it becomes a hard rule. Nothing here is invented on purpose, but unsourced does not mean verified.

Legend: BN = Bangla script, BL = common Banglish spellings (first = standard, rest = typical variants/typos). Entity = what the assistant should map it to.

## Q1. How do shopkeepers name items, and which attribute words matter for lookups?

### Takeaway
Shopkeepers name by brand+model or by a generic Bangla/English loanword, often dropping the brand ("hondar plug", "samsung er set"), and the lookup-critical attributes differ by trade: phones = model + RAM/ROM + colour + condition (new/used) + official/unofficial; auto = vehicle make+model+cc (+year) + part + original/copy origin; hardware = size (mm/inch/feet/gauge) + grade/brand + unit. Web sources confirm product category names and brand+size+unit listing style in print; the spoken glossary is domain knowledge.

### Inferences (domain knowledge, no web source unless stated)

#### Naming habits (all trades)
- Brand-as-generic is common. Examples: "Hondar plug" can mean any bike's plug (uncertain); "Rahimafrooz" is a brand but shoppers say it for a battery in general (uncertain); "Mobil" (মবিল) means engine oil of any brand; "set" (সেট) means a mobile phone (handset), not a kit, in a phone shop; "Walton" can stand for any local fridge/AC/TV (uncertain); "Berger" for any paint (uncertain); "BSRM" for any rod (uncertain). The assistant should treat a brand word with no product as "search brand AND ask/resolve category".
- Possessive pattern: "<brand/model> er <part>" (e.g. "Pulsar er chain set"; "Samsung er charger"; "Akij er tiles"). Parse as model/brand = filter, noun = product.
- "Mal" (মাল) = stock/goods generally ("mal ache?" = is it in stock?). "Piece" in spoken Bangla often "pis/pic".
- Numbers inside names matter and are NOT quantities: "Redmi 12", "A15", "Pulsar 150", "Discover 125", "10 mm rod", "1.5 mm wire". Disambiguate with units (mm/inch/cc/GB/ml/L) and with "ta" (টা) = count marker ("10 ta" = ten pieces).

## Q2. Which words signal how much / how many / left in stock / sold / owed / profit / warranty left, plus units and money words?

### Takeaway
Questions are short and verb-light: price = "dam"; count = "koyta/koto ta"; stock = "ache/stock e/mal"; sold = "bikri/sell"; owed = "baki/pabo/dhar"; profit = "labh/profit"; warranty = "warranty/guarantee/gyarenti". The assistant should treat the unit word after a number as the attribute. "Nogod" is a major trap: it means cash AND is also the name of a mobile wallet (Nagad).

## Q3. Which spellings and typos are common, especially English loanwords written in Bangla script?

### Takeaway
Banglish has no standard spelling: the same word appears with vowel swaps (a/o/e), doubled consonants, dropped letters and phonetic ch/c/j variants. Only a few forms are source-attested (বালু/বালি; Latin "EMI" inside Bangla text; Bangla-script charger forms). The assistant should normalise by phonetic folding plus a synonym table rather than a fixed word list.

## Q4. Which terms are ambiguous (so the assistant must ask a follow-up)? Regional and slang differences, brand-as-generic, model vs name, West Bengal vs Bangladesh

### Takeaway
The riskiest ambiguities are direction of money ("baki", "dhar", "pabo"), cash vs Nagad ("nogod"), quantity vs model numbers ("150", "10"), unit scope (piece/box/set; ton vs kg; litre vs gallon), mobil (engine oil) vs mobile (phone), "set" (phone vs kit), "filter", and "original" (genuine vs original-quality copy). Bangladesh uses MFS names (bKash, Nagad, Rocket), BTRC/NEIR (not PTA), "কিস্তি" and "বালু/বালি"; West Bengal usage differs in payment (UPI, Paytm), regulation (CEIR), and several vocabulary choices.

### Cited Findings
- Sand appears as both বালু and বালি in Bangladeshi sources — [Prothom Alo](https://www.prothomalo.com/business/un1adf221m); [Bikroy](https://bikroy.com/bn/building-materials/concrete)
- Muslim and Bangladeshi Bengali leans more on Persian/Arabic words than Hindu/West Bengal Bengali (general note, shop-talk implications unverified) — [eSamskriti](https://esamskriti.com/e/History/History-Of-Indian-Languages/Bengali-Phrases-Used-In-West-Bengal-And-Bangladesh-1.aspx)

#### Model number vs name (electronics)
- Customers say marketing names (Redmi Note 12, Galaxy A15, iPhone 11) and shorthand ("A15", "note 12", "11 pro max"). Variants: RAM/ROM and region (global/BD) change price and stock; ask only if multiple variants exist.
- Auto: vehicle name+cc+maybe year (Pulsar 150 vs Pulsar 150 Neon; Honda CBR; Discover 125 vs 135). Part numbers are rare in speech.
- Hardware: spec in numbers + unit (10 mm rod, 24x24 tiles, 3.64 L paint).

#### Regional / Dhaka vs Chattogram / slang (all uncertain, no source)
- Chattogram Bengali: pronunciation differs (e.g. s/h shifts) and hardware terms may differ; no verified examples found. Do not hard-code. Use fuzzy matching and learned aliases per shop.
- Dhaka old town ("Kutti") speech may affect spellings; again unverified.
- Sylhet: heavy sand/stone trade ("Sylhet bali"); name used in Dhaka as quality tier (uncertain).
- "Mistri" (মিস্ত্রি) = craftsman/mechanic/electrician/mason: common across trades; ask which.

#### West Bengal / India differences to flag (domain knowledge, not sourced)
- Payments: BD = bKash/Nagad/Rocket/Upay; WB = UPI/GPay/PhonePe/Paytm. Do not map UPI.
- Phone regulation: BD = BTRC/NEIR (sourced); India = CEIR; Pakistan = PTA ("PTA" is not a BD thing, though some BD sellers use it, uncertain).
- Installment: BD "কিস্তি"; India "EMI" is the dominant word (BD also uses EMI in ads).
- Rod: India "TMT bar", BD "রড/শরিয়া"; "Fe500D" (India) vs "grade 60" (BD, sourced via Bangla Tribune snippet).
- Stone chips: WB often "পাথরকুচি/chips"; BD "খোয়া/চিপস" (uncertain).
- Sand: WB mostly "বালি"; BD both "বালু" and "বালি" (sourced).
- Plumbing/sanitary: BD "কমোড" and "প্যান" (sourced SWISH listing), WB similar; WB often uses "WC".
- Paint units: BD paint packs 3.64 L and 18 L drum (sourced); India sells 1/4/10/20 L.
- Cement bag: BD 50 kg (sourced); India also 50 kg.
- Currency: BD "টাকা/tk"; WB "টাকা/Rs" (rupee "রুপি").
