# Bangladeshi shop vocabulary: Fabric & textiles, Fashion & clothing, Footwear & bags

**How to read this file (important for the report writer).**
Web sources on Banglish *shop slang* are very thin. English-language news and marketplace pages give product names, markets, seasons and a few Bangla trade words, but almost nothing on how owners actually type questions. So:
- **Cited Findings** = things I saw in a fetched page (or a search-result summary, flagged where the page was not opened).
- **Inferences** = the glossary itself. Bangla-script forms, Banglish spellings, misspelling patterns and example questions are built from general knowledge of Bangla and Bangla-to-Roman transliteration habits. They are NOT source-verified unless tagged `[S]` (tied to a cited finding). Terms I am less sure of carry **(uncertain)**. No Facebook/Reddit/Quora Banglish corpus was found, so every "typical misspelling" is a phonetic-pattern inference, not an observed count.
- **Gaps** = what could not be verified.

Tag key: `[S]` = backed by a cited finding in the same section. `(uncertain)` = single weak source or my own inference only.

# Bangla / Banglish vocabulary for Fabric & textiles, Fashion & clothing, Footwear & bags retail

## Key question 1: What do shopkeepers call the main product families and their variants (colour, design, size, fabric type)? Plus units, measures, packaging, sizes (objective a, b)

### Takeaway
Fabric traders sell by the **gaj (yard)** off a full **thaan (bolt)**, quote width in **hat/bohor**, and name fabrics by weave/finish (suti, katan, jamdani, silk, jacquard, andi, screen-print). Fashion is organised around garment sets (3-piece, panjabi, fatua, kurti, borka) and sold by piece and size; footwear by **jora (pair)** and numeric size, bags by piece. Product names in Bangla script are mostly English loans written phonetically (e.g. থ্রিপিস, স্ল্যাব কটন, ফ্ল্যাট স্লিপার), so the assistant must map both scripts.

### Cited Findings
- Cloth is priced **per গজ (gaj, yard)** in Dhaka Islampur-side shops, which also stock a full **থান (thaan, bolt)** for panjabi, kurta, salwar-kameez and blouse cloth. Width is **বহর (bohor)** measured in **হাত (hat)**, e.g. "আড়াই হাত" (2.5 hat) and "সাড়ে তিন হাত বহর" (3.5 hat); price rises with width (a tant at 2.5 hat = Tk 220/gaj vs 350/gaj at 3.5 hat). — [Prothom Alo](https://www.prothomalo.com/lifestyle/shopping/syftu6xnj4)
- Same article's fabric/garment words in Bangla script: জ্যাকার্ড প্রিন্ট, জ্যাকার্ড সুতি, অ্যান্ডি সুতি, অ্যান্ডি সিল্ক, রাজশাহী সিল্ক, হাফসিল্ক মসলিন, স্ল্যাব কটন, স্ক্রিন প্রিন্ট, তাঁত, আড়ং সুতি, চায়না সুতি, লিনেন, টাইডাই; garments কামিজ, প্যান্ট, শার্ট, পাঞ্জাবি, কুর্তা, সালোয়ার-কামিজ, ব্লাউজ, ওড়না, থ্রিপিস (sold as a set, ~Tk 800 in that piece). Sample per-gaj prices: screen-print from Tk 140, jacquard cotton 150-200, andi cotton 450-500, andi silk 700-750. — [Prothom Alo](https://www.prothomalo.com/lifestyle/shopping/syftu6xnj4)
- Bangla government dictionary: গজ = a measure of length equal to two cubits, a yard (example "তিন গজ কাপড়"); গজ কাঠি = yardstick for measuring cloth; গজ also means elephant and (chess) bishop. — [Accessible Dictionary: গজ](https://accessibledictionary.gov.bd/bengali-to-english/?q=গজ)
- Bangla government dictionary: থান = "a piece (of cloth etc.)"; থান কাপড় = an entire sheet/length of woven fabric; also an adjective "whole, entire, unbroken" (থান ইট = whole brick). — [Accessible Dictionary: থান](https://accessibledictionary.gov.bd/bengali-to-english/?q=থান)
- Old Dhaka Islampur wholesale market (6,500+ showrooms in ~95 markets; 10,000+ counting makeshift shops) trades gabardine, silk, corduroy, georgette, rayon, polyester, chikankari, cotton mulmul, markin, Bexi voile, printed tissue, "Chinese and local prints"; garments lungis, sarees, shalwar kameez, panjabis, maxi, "Kabli" dress, baby clothes; also curtain/upholstery/sofa-lining material, lace, buttons, stones. — [Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/weekend-read/news/islampur-business-hub-centuries-old-vibe-3562351)

## Key question 2: Which words signal 'how much', 'how many', 'left in stock', 'sold', 'owed', 'profit' in this trade? How do owners and staff phrase questions about sales, stock, price, dues and profit (objective d)?

### Takeaway
The signal words are the same across all three trades: *koto* (amount), *koyta* (count), *ache/nai/shesh/kom* (stock), *bikri/sell* (sold), *baki/pawna* (owed to me), *dena* (I owe), *lav* (profit). The only trade-specific parts are the **unit noun** (gaj/than vs pis/size vs jora/number) and the **product noun**. Two traps: **baki** means both "remaining" and "owed"; **kal** means both "yesterday" and "tomorrow".

### Cited Findings
- দাম কত? (dam koto), কত পড়বে? / কত লাগবে? (how much will it come to), কত টাকা? and "do you have something bigger/smaller…?" are standard shopping phrasings. — [Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Bengali/Shopping)
- Credit is recorded by shopkeepers in a ledger; disputes arise at month-end when the customer is told the due; apps like TallyKhata send an SMS with item price and outstanding balance. Reported due Tk 2,913 disputed vs customer belief under Tk 2,000. — [TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/feature/panorama/tallykhata-digitising-culture-buying-and-selling-credit-154465)
- Bangla fraction words "আড়াই" (2.5) and "সাড়ে তিন" (3.5) appear in real trade copy, so the parser must handle them. — [Prothom Alo](https://www.prothomalo.com/lifestyle/shopping/syftu6xnj4)

#### Disambiguation rules to give the assistant
- **baki + ache/koto** about an item = remaining stock; **baki** + person's name / "kar kache" = owed. "X er baki" (X's baki) = X owes me; "supplier er baki" can mean I owe supplier (say ask-back). Prefer `pawna`/`dena` when seen.
- **kal**: use tense verb (hoyeche/holo = yesterday; hobe/ashbe = tomorrow).
- **sell** (English) vs **sale** vs **bikri**: all = sales.
- **number** (নাম্বার) in footwear = size; **size** also used; "number" in other context = phone number.

## Key question 4: Which English loanwords are universal vs which Bangla-only words are needed? Seasonal / event words (objective f)

### Takeaway
English loanwords are universal for **product, size, finance-app and marketing words** (stock, size, discount, offer, sale, cash, bKash, order, delivery, collection, design, print, three-piece, gown, kurti, sandal, slipper, formal, casual). Bangla-only words are essential for **measures, price, count, stock state, credit and profit** (gaj, thaan, hat, jora, dam/dor, koto/koyta, ache/nai/shesh, baki/dhar/pawna, lav/loksan, hishab, paikari/khucra). Demand is heavily seasonal: Eid-ul-Fitr is by far the biggest (~40% of local fashion-brand profit; ~30-40% of footwear sales), Pohela Boishakh ~10%.

### Cited Findings
- Eid-ul-Fitr accounts for about 40% of leading fashion brands' profits and Pohela Boishakh about 10% (FEAB, as reported in a March 2026 Dhaka Tribune piece; exact URL not retained, **uncertain**); when Boishakh falls close to Eid, shoppers prioritise Eid (Business Post, search summary, **uncertain**). — see [Dhaka Tribune Boishakh](https://www.dhakatribune.com/business/173316/fashion-houses-upbeat-as-pohela-boishakh-promises) for the Boishakh product list.
- Another estimate: Pohela Boishakh plus Eid ≈ 75% of local fashion-brand sales (search summary, **uncertain**). Brands launching Boishakh lines: Aarong, Bishwo Rang, SaRa, Nipun, Clothing, Jeans Factory (summary, **uncertain**).
- Footwear: Eid-ul-Fitr ≈ 30-40% of footwear turnover; Bata attributes ~35% of annual revenue to major festivals (search summaries, **uncertain** which pages; see [Daily Star on Bata](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/news/bata-shoe-sinks-losses-the-first-time-its-58-year-stay-bangladesh-1937989)).
- Islampur: peak season starts about 10 days before Shab-e-Barat and runs to the second week of Ramadan; retailers buy fabric for tailor-made Eid clothes; Ramadan zakat-clothing purchases are worth crores of taka. — [Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/weekend-read/news/islampur-business-hub-centuries-old-vibe-3562351)
- Zakat-season lungi priced about Tk 120-250; most Islampur shops sell out by about the 20th of Ramadan (search summaries, **uncertain**; e.g. [Daily Star zakat clothes](https://www.thedailystar.net/backpage/zakat-clothes-1245712)).
- Eid demand for panjabi, three-piece and sari rises several times in Ramadan versus ordinary months; kids' wear and footwear also up (Financial Express summaries, **uncertain**). — [Financial Express](https://today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/print/eid-shoppers-crowds-swell-but-sales-still-modest-1711994644)
