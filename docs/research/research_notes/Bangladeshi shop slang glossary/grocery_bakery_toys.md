## Key Question 1: What do shopkeepers call the main product families, and which brand-generic habits exist?

### Takeaway
Staple names are short Persian/Arabic/Bangla words (chal, dal, tel, chini, lobon, moyda, atta, dim, dudh) and sweets use a fixed set of ~25 names with many spellings. Retail catalogues (Chaldal) organise the same families in English category names, so an assistant should map shopkeeper words onto a small canonical family list plus a brand-to-generic alias table.

### Cited Findings
- Chaldal (online grocer) groups food as: Fruits & Vegetables; Meat & Fish; Cooking (Spices, Salt & Sugar, Rice, Dal or Lentil, Ready Mix, Shemai & Suji, Special Ingredients, Oil, Colors & Flavours, Ghee, Premium Ingredients); Sauces & Pickles; Dairy & Eggs; Breakfast; Candy & Chocolate; Snacks; Beverages; Baking; Frozen & Canned; plus Toys & Sports as a separate top-level category — [Chaldal rices](https://chaldal.com/rices), [Chaldal home](https://chaldal.com/)
- Rice is sold under variety-style names Chinigura, Nazirshail (also spelled Najirshail), Miniket, usually with brand + weight (e.g. "Teer Chinigura Rice 1 kg", "Teer Nazirshile Rice 5 kg", "Nazirshail Rice Premium Half Boiled 5 kg") — [Teer Chinigura](https://chaldal.com/teer-chinigura-rice-1-kg), [Teer Nazirshile](https://chaldal.com/teer-nazirshile-rice-5-kg)
- Naming caveat: a Bangladesh Post report quotes the Food Minister saying there are no paddy/rice varieties officially named "Miniket" or "Najirshail" and that a law requiring real variety names on bags was planned (undated in results; current status unclear). Treat these as trade names, not botanical names — [Bangladesh Post](https://bangladeshpost.net/posts/miniket-najirshail-rice-bogus-94628)
- Brand-led naming for staples: "Teer Atta 2 kg", "Teer Whole Wheat Atta 2 kg" (atta is named by brand then weight) — [Teer Atta](https://chaldal.com/teer-atta-2-kg), [Teer Whole Wheat Atta](https://chaldal.com/teer-whole-wheat-atta-2-kg)
- Common Bengali word pairs differ Dhaka vs West Bengal: water pani/jol (Dhaka uses both; Shantipur jol), salt lobon (Dhaka) vs nun (Shantipur), chili morich (Dhaka) vs lonka (Shantipur) — [Wikipedia: Bengali vocabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_vocabulary)
- Wikibooks lists shop vocabulary: bajar (market), dokan (shop), kachabajar/mudir dokan (fresh-food market / grocer), farmesi/oshudher dokan (pharmacy) — [Wikibooks Bengali/Shopping](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Bengali/Shopping)
- Sweets (Bangladesh): the Wikipedia list gives 40 items with regional ties, e.g. Amriti (Sylhet; the Daily Star attributes it to Barishal), Chomchom (Tangail/Porabari), Kancha golla (Natore), Pantua (Manikganj), Pranohora (Natore), Roshogolla (listed Barisal), Roshkodom (Rajshahi), Roshomolai (Cumilla), Roshomonjuri (Rangpur), Sandesh (Satkhira, several types), Shahi jilapi (Old Dhaka), Mishti doi / Bogurar doi (Bogra), Chanar jilapi (Dhaka), Chanamukhi (Brahmanbaria), Kalojam (= gulab jamun), Lyangcha, Ledikeni, Kheer er chop, Rabri, Rajbhog, Sitabhog, Sarbhaja, Sarpuria, Pitha — [Wikipedia: Bangladeshi sweets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Bangladeshi_sweets_and_desserts), [Daily Star: sweets history](https://www.thedailystar.net/life-living/news/rich-history-and-tradition-our-sweets-3296651)
- Banglapedia spellings/associations: rasagolla (Phulia first; Savar soft version), chamcham (Porabari, Tangail), manda (Muktagachha), pyada and rasmalai (Comilla), malaikari (Faridpur), kanchagolla (Natore), raskadam/raskadamba (Rajshahi), Bogra dai, amrti and jilipi and pranhara (Dhaka); ladycanny and kalojam are related; sarbhaja/sarpuriya/sitabhog/mihidana are West Bengal (Krishnanagar/Burdwan) specialties — [Banglapedia: Sweetmeats](https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Sweetmeats)
- Bakery item names in retail listings: pauruti (bread), butter bun, burger bun, dry cake, pound cake, cream biscuit, salt biscuit, milk biscuit, almond biscuit, rusk (plain/coconut/spicy/garlic), toast (butter/milk/garlic), ruti, naan (butter/garlic/keema), bakarkhani (thick spiced biscuit-like flatbread, Old Dhaka), morobba ruti — [Othoba bread](https://othoba.com/bread), [Daily Star Ispahani Bakery](https://www.thedailystar.net/life-living/food-recipes/news/ispahani-bakery-fresh-the-ultimate-snack-solution-2946366), [DBpedia Bakarkhani](https://dbpedia.org/page/Bakarkhani)
- Toy trade: Chawkbazar (Dhaka) holds one of the country's largest wholesale toy markets; traders buy from factories in Kamrangirchar — [bdnews24](https://bdnews24.com/media-en/image/x2el6uy2mr). Eid toy items seen in press: plastic toys, whistles, balloons; Eidi (gift money) is given with or instead of gifts — [Dhaka Tribune: Children and Eid joy](https://www.dhakatribune.com/amp/feature/405633/children-and-eid-joy), [Daily Star: Eid gifts](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/eid-gifts)
- Ramadan/iftar goods: piyaju, beguni, jilapi, muri, haleem; chhola (chickpea) bhuna, potato chop, pakowra; Old Dhaka lists also include doi bora, borhani, falooda, chana matha, kebab, sherbets; iftar is also called "roja kholai" (a Dhaka usage in a press report) — [Wikipedia: Iftar](https://Wikipedia.com/wiki/Iftar), [Financial Express](https://today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/print/iftar-items-aplenty-in-city-bazars), [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/dhaka/375201/dhaka-streets-abuzz-with-iftar-delicacies-on)

## Key Question 2: Which words signal "how much", "how many", "left in stock", "sold", "owed", and "profit"? (includes units, money/trade words and example sentences)

### Takeaway
Intent can be detected from a small set of trigger words (koto, koyta, ache/nai/shesh, bikri/becha, baki/dhar/pabo, lav/munafa) combined with a subject type (item vs person vs time word). The critical trap is "baki": it means both "remaining" (stock) and "owed" (dues), resolved by whether the subject is an item or a person. Units are mixed metric (kg, ltr) and traditional (ser, pao, hali, gonda, mon).

### Cited Findings

## Key Question 3: What are the common Banglish spellings, abbreviations and typos?

### Takeaway
Banglish has no standard orthography: the same word varies by vowel doubling (dal/daal), aspirate dropping (dudh/dud, khata/kata), s/sh, j/z, ch/chh, o/u/a swaps, and English-loan spellings (soyabin/soyabean). Matching should use a phonetic normaliser plus a curated alias table, not exact strings.

### Cited Findings
- Sources show the same Bangla word romanised several ways even within reputable sites: nazirshail / najirshail / nazirshile (Chaldal product slugs and Bangladesh Post), roshogolla / rasagolla / rasgulla, jilapi / jilipi / jolbhora variants (Wikipedia list, Banglapedia), chanamukhi / chhanamukhi, chomchom / chamcham, shorbhaja / sarbhaja — [Chaldal rices](https://chaldal.com/rices), [Bangladesh Post](https://bangladeshpost.net/posts/miniket-najirshail-rice-bogus-94628), [Wikipedia sweets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Bangladeshi_sweets_and_desserts), [Banglapedia Sweetmeats](https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Sweetmeats)
- Wikibooks uses a scholarly romanisation (dôjon, pãchsho, ṭaka, keji) that differs from what shopkeepers type (dojon, pachsho, taka, kejee) — [Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Bengali/Shopping)
- Bangladeshi fintech apps write English loanwords in Bangla script (সাপ্লায়ার, রিটেইলার, মোবাইল রিচার্জ), so the same business word appears as supplier / সাপ্লায়ার — [TallyKhata](https://www.tallykhata.com/en/home-eng/)

## Key Question 4: Which words need Bangla-script handling vs plain Banglish? (includes regional/ambiguous terms and seasonal vocabulary)

### Takeaway
Typed input will usually be Banglish with English units/loans in Latin; voice input (STT) will usually return Bangla script, with English loanwords transliterated into Bangla script (প্যাকেট, ডিসকাউন্ট, স্টক). Handle both by normalising to a phonetic key. Regional vocabulary differences (Dhaka vs Chattogram vs Sylhet vs West Bengal) are real but sparsely documented: only a handful of confirmed pairs exist; most regional claims here are flagged (uncertain).

#### A. Script handling matrix
| Input type | Expect | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Typed Banglish (Latin) | item/person names in Banglish; units kg/ltr/pkt/pcs; amounts in Latin digits | phonetic key + alias table (see Q3) |
| Typed Bangla script | proper Bangla spellings with common slips (ন/ণ, ি/ী) | Unicode NFC normalise, strip vowel signs mismatches for matching; map Bangla digits |
| Voice (STT) | Bangla script; English loans in Bangla script (স্টক, প্যাকেট, ডিসকাউন্ট, কার্টন, লিটার, বিল, মেমো) or sometimes English; numbers sometimes as words ("দুই হাজার") or digits | transliterate all to one key; maintain loan-word list: স্টক=stock, প্যাকেট=packet, ডিসকাউন্ট=discount, কার্টন=carton, কেজি=kg, লিটার=litre, বোতল=bottle, ডজন=dozen, পিস=piece, বিকাশ=bkash, নগদ=nogod/Nagad, ক্যাশ=cash, প্রফিট=profit, ড্যু=due |
| Items needing Bangla-script awareness | Bangla-only terms with no standard Latin: দেড় (1.5), আড়াই (2.5), সাড়ে (+0.5), আধা (half), হালি, গণ্ডা, কুড়ি, সের, পোয়া, মণ; sweets (ক্ষীর, পায়েস) | explicit lookup table for fractions/numerals; do not rely on Latin matching |
| Items safe in plain Banglish | brand names (Pran, Teer, Fresh, Radhuni, Ispahani), English product words (biscuit, juice, noodles, cake, bun, teddy, remote car, battery), units (kg, ltr, pkt, ctn, pcs), "discount", "stock", "profit", "bkash" | exact + fuzzy |
| Names of people | Bangla or Latin; honorifics bhai/apa/chacha/mama/khala/sir/madam/dada; "Rahim bhai", "Karim chacha" | store both script versions; strip honorific for matching; transliteration fuzzy |

#### B. Regional / dialect & slang / West Bengal differences
- **[WB] confirmed pairs:** pani/jol (BD uses both, Muslim speech skews pani), lobon vs nun, morich vs lonka. 
- **[WB] other likely differences (uncertain):** BD "torkari" for vegetables vs WB "sabji"; BD "peyaj" vs WB "peyanj"; BD "mach/mangsho" same; BD "dal/dim/chini" same; BD "tel" same; WB uses "dokan" and "bazar" too. In Bangladesh, Persian/Arabic-origin words such as munafa, dokan, hisab, bakhsish, mojud, bhara, mohajon are common and the Sanskrit-ish "labh"/"nirdharito" forms more bookish (uncertain).
- **Dhaka vs Chattogram vs Sylhet (uncertain, not found in sources):** Sylhet: maas (fish), shuṭki, shorisha (confirmed); Sylhet's citrus "satkora/hatkora" (uncertain), Sylheti "ful" for flower (uncertain). Chattogram: Chittagong "shutki" culture and "mezban" (মেজবান, a beef feast; uncertain). Fish and spice names differ locally (e.g. regional names for rui, koi, puti, shing, magur) but no verified word list was located. Instruct the assistant to treat unknown local names via fuzzy matching to the shop's own catalogue, not a hard-coded dictionary.
- **Dhaka vs Old Dhaka ("Dhakaiya") slang:** "kutti" style slang (uncertain; skip); "roja kholai" (iftar, sourced), "shahi" prefix = rich/royal (shahi jilapi, shahi haleem, shahi doi bora).
- **Words with different meanings across trades:** "pau/pao" (bread vs quarter ser); "hali" (4 eggs vs 5 bananas/mangoes vs 7 fish by district); "kuri" (20 vs 22-25); "mal" (goods in all trades; "ready mal" = ready stock, uncertain); "baki" (owed vs remaining); "set" (kitchen set toys vs "set menu" food); "piece/pis" (one unit; for sweets and bakery often means 1 item, for toys 1 unit); "cell" (battery); "box" (a gift box vs a sweet box with a standard qty); "kg" (sweets by weight vs veg by weight); "doz/dozen" (eggs vs cake pieces); "pound" (cake size, not currency; uncertain); "dalda" (any ghee); "doi/dohi" (curd vs Bogurar doi, a brand-like product); "rosh/ros" (juice vs sweet syrup, "ros" রস); "tel" (cooking vs hair vs kerosene); "chini" (sugar vs chinigura rice); "mishti" (sweet taste vs sweets product); "cake" (cake vs dry cake vs rusk). Toys: "original/copy/first copy", "local" vs "import" vs "china" are quality flags rather than origins.
- **Pronoun/politeness register:** apni (formal), tumi (neutral), tui (rude/intimate); shopkeeper staff talk uses tumi/tui with owners using apni; do not rely on pronouns for intent.
