# Teaching SortPi to Separate Asking from Ordering

**Summary.** A SortPi phone assistant can safely understand Bangladeshi owners if it first decides what kind of sentence it heard, and only then looks for data. The notes support six utterance types: data question, command to show/list/search/send, advice or decision question, action that changes records or moves money, "why" explanation question, and correction or cancel. Three signals separate them: a question word (koto, kon, ke, keno), the verb mood (imperative -o/-un, past -lam/-lo, future -bo/-ben, negation na/ni), and whether the sentence names money, goods and a party. Read-only commands are the safe starting point, because the polite forms (tui, tumi, apni) change only the verb ending, never the meaning, and a bare data noun ("baki", "stock") already means "show it". Advice questions ("dam barabo ki?", "baki dibo ki?") can be answered only for the part the owner's own records support (stock against a reorder level, a customer's balance and age of dues, cost against price), and the decision must go back to the owner. Actions are the danger zone: direction of money is computed from four inputs (the give/take verb, the person ending, the postposition on the party, and the party's role and balance), and one vowel (dilam "I gave" versus dilo "he/she gave") reverses the entry. The notes therefore recommend that every write goes through a confirmation card in the first version, with hard stops that turn missing or conflicting facts into questions. **Most of the phrasing in this report is model knowledge, not verified: the researchers' web search found no corpus of real shopkeeper messages, no in-app Bangla labels for TallyKhata, Hishabee, Dokani or bKash, and no source describing advice or correction phrases at all. Every word list, spelling variant and example below is a hypothesis to test with real Bangladeshi shop staff before it becomes a hard rule.**

Where sources or forms conflict (for example -re versus -ke, or দিব versus দেব), both are shown and the conflict is left open, as the notes left it.

This report builds on the earlier glossary report, "Bangladeshi shop slang glossary", which defined the shared question words (koto, koyta, ki/কি, kon, ke/kar, kobe, keno, ache/nai), the data nouns, the "kal" rule and an ambiguity table. Those are used here without repeating them at length. Its ambiguity table settles the following defaults, all proposals rather than verified facts: baki with an item subject or "ache/nai" nearby means remaining stock, and with a person name or "khata" means dues; "ami pabo" means owed to me and "o pabe" means I owe; "nogod" is cash unless "nagad e/theke/number" marks the wallet; and "kal" resolves by verb tense, with a question to the user when still unclear (past marker means yesterday, future marker means tomorrow, no verb means yesterday for data and a question for plans or orders). The sources behind that glossary report conflict in places (month lengths of the Bangla calendar, weights such as hali and pon), and those conflicts are not touched here.

## Six utterance types, and the markers that separate them

The notes describe three groups of cues, and the sixth type comes from the correction notes. A data question carries a quantity or identity word (koto, koyta, ke ke, kon, kar kache, kobe, ache/nai) and no first-person future verb. A command is an imperative, or a "-te hobe / -ye dao" frame. An advice question has a first-person future verb with ki or na ki, an evaluative predicate such as uchit or bhalo hobe, or an explicit request word such as poramorsho, suggestion or advice den. An explanation question carries keno, ki karone, karon ki or kivabe. These cue sets are [MK]. The one confirmed grammar fact behind them is that polite requests are often built from "-ben" plus "ki": the FSI course gives "Apni ki amake sahayyo korben?" (will you help me?) and "Apni ki eta korte parben?" (can you do this?), so the -ben + ki pattern is a request form and not a plain future ([FSI Bengali, unit 6](https://www.fsi-language-courses.org/fsi-bengali-course/6-making-requests-and-offers/)).

Two further kinds of sentence look like actions and are not. A report in the passive ("bikri hoyeche", "mal eseche") has no actor and is fit to record, but still goes through a card. A future promise ("500 pabo", "order dibo", "kal diye dibo") is not a ledger movement: the action notes say to treat it at most as a reminder or "record as due", never as a payment. Likewise "mal nei / shesh" ("out of stock") describes a state and must not subtract anything. The action notes insist that tense and mood classification (command, past report, future promise, question, negated) must come before entity extraction.

The advice notes add a guard rule that decides the whole safety design: an advice-shaped sentence must never trigger a write (price change, order, message send, dues reminder) on its own. "dam barabo ki?" is a question; "dam barai dao" is the command ([MK] design inference). A practical order of checks follows from the notes (this precedence is a design inference, not a finding): first look for negation or hedges and for a question mark (na, noy, thak, pore, dekhi), then for explanation markers, then for the advice frames, then for past or perfect verbs with a party and amount, and only then for imperatives and bare nouns.

Two parsing traps cut across the types. The first is "ki/কি/কী". One Banglish spelling covers both the "what" word (কী) and the yes/no particle (কি). The notes' heuristic, which is [MK] and unverified on real data, is that "what" sits just before the verb ("ki kinbo", "ki korbo") and asks for an option list, while the yes/no particle sits after the verb or at the end ("kinbo ki", "korbo ki") and asks for yes or no. Users often write কি for both. Both are advice-seeking, so the practical consequence is only the reply shape. The second trap is "na". At the end of an imperative it softens ("dekhao na" = please show), before the verb or in "-io na" it forbids ("na dekhao", "dio na"), and for money actions "koro na" can be either "please do" or "do not do". The notes mark the tone distinction as (uncertain), so any imperative that ends in na near a write verb must stop and be shown on a card, never executed.

## The command-verb reference: three politeness levels, many spellings, one intent

The three-level imperative is confirmed by opened grammar sources for three verbs: বলা (tui বল্, tumi বলো, apni বলুন), দেওয়া (দে, দাও, দিন) and খাওয়া (খা, খাও, খান) ([Wikipedia: Bengali grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_grammar)). The FSI course shows the same pattern in romanisation: bos / bosho / bosun, de / dao / din, aash / aasho / asun ([FSI Bengali, unit 6](https://www.fsi-language-courses.org/fsi-bengali-course/6-making-requests-and-offers/)). Wikipedia adds that a plural command changes the pronoun, not the verb ending. Wiktionary gives দেখানো ("to show; to make see"), whose example is আমাকে সবকিছু দেখাও ("Show me everything"), and notes it is the causative of দেখা ([Wiktionary দেখানো](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/দেখানো)). The page for দেখা lists only indicative forms (tui দেখিস, tumi দেখ(o), apni দেখেন; future দেখবি, দেখবে, দেখবেন) and has no imperative row ([Wiktionary দেখা](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/দেখা)). Which level an owner uses with a phone assistant is not known. The notes guess that tumi is the default (the register between owner, staff and a trusted helper), apni is the polite choice, and tui is a close or annoyed tone, and they advise replying in apni forms and never in tui. All of that is [MK], and the notes found no source quantifying register choice. The key parsing fact is the same for every level: the stem and the object decide the intent, and the ending only changes politeness. The notes' example family is "stock dekh" (tui), "stock dekho" (tumi), "stock dekhun" (apni), "stock dekhaben?" (polite future), "stock ektu dekhan", "stock dekhiye den", "stock dekhte chai" and "stock dekha jabe?", which all mean SHOW. Because the apni imperative of the causative (দেখান, "show me") differs from the plain verb's (দেখুন, "look yourself") by one vowel, typists swap them, and the notes tell the parser to treat the two as the same intent. They also note that the apni present, future and imperative share forms (দেখেন, দেখবেন, দেখান/দেখুন), so in speech "den", "diben" or "bolen" can all be requests when the sentence has no subject and ends in "?" or "to"; classification should rest on the missing subject plus the data noun, not on tense.

Wishes and question-shaped requests carry the same payload as imperatives, and the notes list them as show or tell intents: dekhte chai / দেখতে চাই, jante chai / জানতে চাই, amar X dorkar / X lagbe / X chai (show or give X), dekhi / dekhi to / দেখি তো (soft show, "let me see"), ektu dekhaben? / একটু দেখাবেন?, bolte parben?, dekhano jabe?, and "ache?" or "hobe?" as a check rather than an order. Politeness and softening words are stripped before parsing. "doya kore" (kindly) is confirmed in the FSI course ("Doya kore eta din", "Doya kore asun") but the notes say shopkeepers rarely use it and that ektu, pls/plz and please do the same job in practice [MK]. Address terms (bhai, apu, sir, boss, mama, chacha) and greetings ("assalamu alaikum", "bhai", "ektu") appear before the real request; the one Bangladeshi dataset the advice researcher could open, BanglaEComIntent, shows a greeting plus "vai" ahead of price questions such as "assalamu alaikum vai ei saree tar dam koto" ([BanglaEComIntent](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Badhon/BanglaEComIntent)).

The typing patterns the notes list ([MK]) are o to u/w (dekhao, dekhau, dekhaw), vowel dropping in chat (dkhao, dekao, ptao), the apni ending spelled -en, -un or -an, aspirate collapse (khujo to kujo, as BanglaTLit shows for bhalo/valo/balo), autocorrect to English ("den" becoming "then" or "Dao"), and Bangla-keyboard slips (দেখাও to দেখাউ). The spoken polite forms দেন and দিবেন (and "dein" as a typo) can also act as imperatives. Another cited fact bears on dictation: an Indian-language transliteration study counted an average of 3.8 attested romanisations per Bengali word ([Dakshina paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.01176), via the earlier report), which is why matching should use a phonetic skeleton, not exact strings. The datasets the notes found do not help with commands directly. The BanglaLP paper (Sakib et al. 2023) has 984 samples, 10 intents and three varieties (formal, colloquial, Sylheti), but its data are machine-translated English SNIPS samples with intents like BookRestaurant and GetWeather, so they are not spontaneous commands and have nothing about shops; GPT-3.5 reached F1 0.94 on intent but only 0.51 on slots for colloquial Bangla ([arXiv 2310.10935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10935)). BanglaEComIntent has 15 intents including out_of_scope, but no advice, "why" or action-style intent. IndoTranslit (975,215 synthetic romanised pairs) and BNIntent30 were seen in search summaries only.

### The bare-noun pattern

Bangladeshi apps overwhelmingly use the polite -উন / -ন imperative on buttons (দেখুন, পাঠান, নিন, যোগ করুন, সেভ করুন, মুছুন, বাতিল করুন), with English loan nouns plus করুন for newer actions. The verified strings are limited to what the TallyKhata site shows: "ভিডিও দেখুন", "টালিপে QR নিন", "ডাউনলোড টালিখাতা", "অ্যাড মানি, মানি আউট", and the feature nouns "বাকি বেচা, কেনা, খরচ, স্টক" ([TallyKhata](https://www.tallykhata.com/)). The store titles show the same style: "ব্যবসা বাড়ান" (Hishabee, title only) and "ব্যবসা চালান সহজে" (TallyKhata, via an AppGoblin listing, [Play Store Hishabee](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.hishabee.business&hl=bn), [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.progoti.tallykhata)). The TallyKhata page lacked "যোগ করুন", "বিক্রি", "রিপোর্ট" and an exact "পাঠান". The Play Store pages for TallyKhata, Hishabee and bKash returned titles only, the bKash site returned 403 and Hishabee's home page failed, so no verbatim in-app button list was read, and the label table in the notes is largely [MK]. The notes also found no evidence on how shopkeepers actually type to any app; none of the apps opened has a chat or command input.

The notes nonetheless argue, as [MK], that because tabs are nouns (স্টক, বাকি, বিক্রি / বেচা, কেনা, খরচ, হিসাব, রিপোর্ট, ক্যাশ), an owner who sees "বাকি" as a tab will type "baki". The bare-noun forms they list: a noun alone ("stock", "baki", "bikri", "khoroch", "hisab", "lav", "joma") means "show current X"; noun + dekhao/koto ("stock dekhao", "baki koto"); time + noun ("aj bikri", "ei maser lav"); name + noun ("Rahim er baki", "Karim bhai r baki koto"); and noun + "?" ("stock?"). Word order is free: "stock dekhao", "dekhao stock" and "dekhao to stock ta" all occur, so the parser should match verb stem, noun slot and modifiers separately. Scope modifiers (মোট, সব, সর্বমোট, এখন, আমার, আজকের, গতকাল, এই মাসের, আগের, শুধু, সর্বশেষ, "mot koto", "sob mile") attach to the noun and change the scope, not the verb. One bare-noun hazard is stated outright: a name plus a number with no verb ("Karim 500") has unknown direction and must be asked about, because the "show current X" default applies only to data nouns, never to a name and an amount.

## The advice reference: markers, topics, hedges, and what the data can answer

The advice researcher found no source that describes these constructions. The only Bangladeshi intent dataset opened, BanglaEComIntent, contains no advice or recommendation class and no such utterances ([BanglaEComIntent](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Badhon/BanglaEComIntent)); the Rasa-based Bangla "business assistant" paper lists no intents or example utterances and says annotated data were not available ([arXiv 2107.05541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05541v4)); and the FSI course has "ki + verb" offer frames ("Ki korte pari?", "Apni ki ... parben?", "Jodi apni paren, ekhane bosun") but no "shall I / should I" frames ([FSI Bengali, unit 6](https://www.fsi-language-courses.org/fsi-bengali-course/6-making-requests-and-offers/)). A search-summary-only note gives the future forms korbo (I), korbe (informal you) and korben (formal you) ([FSI Bengali ch.23](https://www.fsi-language-courses.org/fsi-bengali-course/23-future-plans-and-intentions/), snippet only). Everything below is [MK] and should be checked against real messages. A related finding from the datasets matters for evaluation: a naive split inflates accuracy to about 99.9% by memorising templates, so an advice class needs template-level splits and real messages ([BanglaEComIntent](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Badhon/BanglaEComIntent)).

### Hedges and wrappers

Hedges wrap an intent and do not decide it. "bolen to" (বলেন তো) can open an advice question or a plain data question ("bolen to koto baki ache"). "mone hoy" and "apnar ki mone hoy?" request an opinion; "ektu bujhiye den" asks for an explanation, which signals explanation more than advice; "doya kore / please ... din" is the polite request frame confirmed by FSI. Address terms and greetings are stripped before classification. The Indian-publisher Bangla pages the researcher opened use "পরামর্শ" (poramorsho) and the English loan "টিপস" (tips) in headings, so both are normal in published Bangla, but the register is WB/India and BD owners are more likely to say suggestion or advice ([Khatabook bn](https://khatabook.com/blog/bn/%E0%A6%9B%E0%A7%8B%E0%A6%9F-%E0%A6%AC%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%AC%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%B0-%E0%A6%9F%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%AA%E0%A6%B8/)).

### What data can safely answer, and what must be declined

"Safe" means a lookup or arithmetic on the owner's own data, with no prediction. This table is the advice researcher's design reasoning [MK], not a sourced finding.

A search for credit-limit practice returned only generic English and Indian guidance (a per-customer limit with an alert near the limit, written terms, scheduled reminders), so the limit and reorder-level rules above must be set by the owner, since no Bangladesh source shows how shops set them.

### "Why" questions and look-alike ranking questions

Explanation markers [MK]: কেন (keno, kano, kno, ken), ক্যান (kyan, colloquial and sometimes chiding: "keno ar baki dibo?"), কী কারণে / কারণ কী (ki karone, karon ki), কীভাবে (kivabe, kibhabe, kibabe), কেমনে (kemne), মানে কী (mane ki), কোথায় গেল (kothay gelo), বুঝিয়ে বলেন. FSI has no "keno/kivabe" frames and BanglaEComIntent has no "why" intent, so these are unsourced. The notes distinguish a "why" from the ranking questions that look like advice but are data: "kon mal e labh beshi" (margin or profit ranking), "konta beshi bikri hoy" (top sellers), "kar kache shobcheye beshi baki" (who owes most), and "taka kothay gelo" (a breakdown of expenses, stock purchases and outstanding baki). The tell-apart rule is that "kon ... beshi/shobcheye" without a first-person future verb is a ranking query, while the same words plus korbo/kinbo/dibo ki is advice. "kon customer shobcheye bhalo" is ambiguous (highest spend, or best payer?), so the assistant asks or shows both numbers and never rates character. A "why" answer may state only what the owner's own records show: fewer sales, a smaller basket, an item out of stock for N days, a price change on a date, a discount that ran. Outside causes (competitors, hartal, rain, rice prices) are listed only as "not in my data, I cannot tell". "keno" can also be rhetorical or a complaint ("keno ami baki dibo?"); a question mark, a following number word or a comparison ("age-r cheye") suggests the data reading.

### Reply shapes

The notes' reply shape for an advice question [MK design suggestion] has three parts: answer the part the data supports with numbers and the rule used; name what the data cannot tell (the future, other shops, people); and hand the decision back ("apni thik korun"). Phrasing such as "apnar uchit ..." must not appear on any declined row. For a ranking or "why" question the shape is numbers first, then "what I can see" and "what I cannot see". Whether owners accept a reply that says "I can only show the numbers" is untested.

### Verbs for payments, credit, sales, returns, stock and edits

Three light verbs do most of the work: দেওয়া (give), নেওয়া (take), করা (do), plus পাওয়া (get), combined with a money or goods noun. Wiktionary confirms the give paradigm: past দিলাম (dilam, I), দিলে (you), দিল (dilo, he/she/they), দিলেন (polite); perfect দিয়েছি (I), দিয়েছ (you), দিয়েছে (he/she), দিয়েছেন (polite); future দিব/দেব, দিবে/দেবে, দিবেন/দেবেন; and it works as an auxiliary meaning to finish doing something ([Wiktionary দেওয়া](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A6%A6%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%93%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE)). It confirms the take paradigm: নিলাম, নিলে, নিল, নিলেন; perfect নিয়েছি, নিয়েছ, নিয়েছে, নিয়েছেন; future নিব/নেব; imperatives নিস, নাও, নেন ([Wiktionary নেওয়া](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A6%A8%E0%A7%87%E0%A6%93%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE)). It confirms that বাকি (baki) means both "remainder" and "due or debt" (example: "bakita amake dao" = give me the rest) ([Wiktionary বাকি](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A6%AC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%95%E0%A6%BF)), and lists ধার (dhar) as "debt or loan" without verb usage ([Wiktionary ধার](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A6%A7%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B0)). | Bill, sale | বিল করো / বিল কাটো / মেমো দাও (bill koro, bill kato, memo dao); বিক্রি করো; বিক্রি হয়েছে (bikri hoyeche / hoise) | create a bill or memo (memo is common in BD [MK]); "bikri hoyni" = not sold | "bill dao" can also be a customer asking for a bill; "X ke bill dao" is a sale, not a payment |
| Return | ফেরত নাও / নিলাম (ferot nao, ferot nilam, pherot) = take back (goods IN, usually money or credit OUT); ফেরত দিলাম / দাও = gave back goods to a supplier, money to a customer, or change ("baki poisha ferot dao") | three meanings; never act on "ferot" alone | subject decides |
| Exchange | এক্সচেঞ্জ / বদল (exchange, bodol) | two goods lines plus a difference that can move either way | card needed |
| Cancel, undo, erase | ক্যান্সেল / বাতিল করো (batil koro); মুছে দাও / ডিলিট করো (muche dao, delete koro, fele dao) | needs an object, usually "the previous/last one"; "fele dao" also means throw away goods | "ulte dao" as undo is (uncertain) |
| Stock in | মাল এসেছে / মাল পেলাম (mal eseche, mal pelam); মাল নামালাম (mal namalam) | goods arrived or unloaded: stock IN | cost and whether bought on baki are not stated |
| Stock out | মাল গেল (mal gelo); মাল নষ্ট (mal nosto, mal faltu) | sold, lost, stolen or given, reason unknown; spoiled or junk (a write-off, but "faltu" can be just an opinion) | reason and qty required |
| Ambiguous stock | মাল তুলেছি (mal tulechi) | either loaded goods (out) or put goods up in the shop (in); (uncertain) | always ask; whether "mal tola" means in or out in BD shops is unresolved |
| State, not event | মাল নেই / শেষ (mal nei, shesh) | out of stock | do not subtract |
| Orders | অর্ডার দাও (order dao) = place an order; "order dilam" = I placed one; "order nilam" = I took one from a customer; অর্ডার দিবো = future | party roles reversed between the last two | "order dibo" is at most a draft or reminder |
| Count | স্টক গণনা / মাল গুনে দেখো (stock gonona, mal gune dekho) | a prompt to count | the count then SETs quantities (overwrite), which is high risk |
| Master data | নতুন মাল যোগ করো (notun mal jog koro); কাস্টমার অ্যাড করো; দাম বদলাও / কমাও / বাড়াও (dam bodlao, komao, barao); নাম বদলাও | add product or customer, change price, rename | "jog koro" also means add to a total; "5 ta jog koro" is add 5 pieces |

A Bangla-titled dues app, "ব্যবসার হিসাব – বাকি খাতা", has an English listing ([AppGoblin listing](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.bbz.bebsharhisab)). TBS News describes credit one-directionally (customers owe the shop, each credit purchase is entered, and the customer gets an SMS of price and balance) ([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/feature/panorama/tallykhata-digitising-culture-buying-and-selling-credit-154465)). A Daily Star supplement is reported to show a customer SMS "Today's purchase 320 BDT, total due 1170 BDT" ([Daily Star supplement PDF](https://epfs.thedailystar.net/TDS/2024/05/30/Bangladesh/SUP/5_02/78bab4010af163b5_02.pdf), snippet only).

### How direction is computed

Direction is a computation from four inputs, and if any is missing or in conflict the assistant must ask rather than guess: (1) the give or take verb (দেওয়া, নেওয়া, পাওয়া); (2) the person ending (-lam for I/we, -lo for he/she/they, -len polite); (3) the postposition on the party (ke marks the recipient; theke or "er kach theke" marks the source); and (4) the party's role and existing balance (customer, supplier, staff, self). Wikipedia supports the case system: genitive -র/-এর, objective -কে in West Bengal and -রে common in Bangladesh, ablative থেকে, postpositions after the noun ([Wikipedia: Bengali grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_grammar)). The conflict between -ke and -re is left as the notes left it: "Karimre 500 taka dilam" may appear in spoken Dhaka-style Banglish, so the tokenizer must treat -re and -ke alike and strip the suffix when matching names. Wikibooks confirms "apnar kachhe" (with you / in your possession) as the ordinary possession construction ([Wikibooks Bengali/Shopping](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Bengali/Shopping)).

| Surface pattern (speaker is the shop owner or staff) | Reads as | Money | Account effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| X ke N taka dilam | I gave X N | OUT | customer: lent more or refund; supplier: reduces what I owe; staff: advance or salary |
| X er kach theke N taka niyechi | I took N from X | IN | customer: reduces what X owes me (or a cash sale); supplier: refund or borrowing; lender: I borrowed |
| X N taka dilo / diyeche | X gave me N | IN | as above |
| X ke N taka dibo | I owe or will pay X | none now | future payable or reminder |
| X er kach theke N taka pabo | I will get N from X | none now | future receivable |
| X ke N taka dhar dilam | I lent X | OUT if cash | X owes me more |
| X er kach theke N taka dhar nilam | I borrowed | IN | I owe X |
| X er baki N | X's due is N | none | usually X owes me; if X is a supplier, I owe X |
| X shodh korlo / shodh korlam | X paid off / I paid off | IN / OUT | owes less / I owe less |

The notes rank the dangerous ambiguities by the loss if wrong. First is dilam versus dilo versus dilen (and nilam, nilo, nilen): one vowel in Banglish, and speech-to-text may normalise the ending, so the card must restate the interpretation in words with an arrow. Second is subject drop: "500 taka dilo" has no subject, "Karim 500 dilo" has the subject Karim, and "500 dilo Karim ke" would mean someone gave to Karim; the two Karim readings are opposite. Third is ke versus theke versus er kach theke, with ke also being the question word কে ("ke dilo?" = who gave?), and theke also marking a from-value ("120 theke 100 koro"). Fourth is baki as remainder, due or change: "Baki 400 dao" can mean give me the remaining 400 (change) or pay the 400 still owed, and the checksum total minus paid equals baki is the guard. Fifth, dhar versus baki versus joma: credit-on-goods is baki, lending cash is "dhar dilam", joma is a payment in the customer's ledger, and mixed sentences ("Rahim er 500 baki joma koro") may mean one entry or two. Sixth, pabo/dibo are future (a promise or reminder), yet "500 pabo" is often used for a balance statement ("he owes me 500"); treat as "set/adjust baki" only after explicit confirmation of which. The remaining ones: "X ke bill dao" is a sale; a name before "diye" is a channel or intermediary, not the payee ("bkash diye dilo" is by bKash; "Karim diye pathalam" is through Karim); a chain such as "Rahim er hoye Karim dilo" (Karim paid on behalf of Rahim) is rare and should be rejected and asked; and joma as a bank deposit takes cash out of the till without being an expense to the customer ledger. The earlier glossary report's default "ami pabo = owed to me; o pabe = I owe" is a proposal, not a verified rule, and the action notes have no source for it either.

### Amounts, units and party order

Typical order is Party(+case) - amount(+currency or unit) - verb, but order is free and afterthought repetition occurs. Bangla digit and number-word facts are cited from Wikipedia: ০ to ৯ with the words শূন্য, এক, দুই, তিন, চার, পাঁচ, ছয়, সাত, আট, নয়; irregular 10 to 19; tens including বিশ/কুড়ি, ত্রিশ/তিরিশ, চল্লিশ, পঞ্চাশ, ষাট, সত্তর, আশি, নব্বই; 100 শত/একশ, 1,000 হাজার, 100,000 লক্ষ/লাখ, 10 million কোটি; and the fraction prefixes সাড়ে (+1/2), সোয়া (+1/4), পৌনে (-1/4) ([Wikipedia Bengali numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_numerals)). That article did not cover দেড়, আড়াই or ডজন. Classifiers -টা, -টি and -জন are cited from Wikipedia's grammar page. Wikibooks supplies সের (about 0.933 kg), পোয়া, হালি (4), ডজন (12), হাফ ডজন, আধা কেজি, "aro ekta", "kono bhangti nai" and question forms like "dam kôto?" ([Wikibooks Bengali/Shopping](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Bengali/Shopping)). A snippet-only note says Bangla spoken-numeral recognition work covers only 0 to 99 (about 89.6% for a CNN on one dataset) with no standard speech corpus, and compound large numbers such as hajar and shoto were not covered ([BanglaNum, arXiv 2403.13465](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13465v1)).

The sentence frames the notes expect, in order of assumed frequency [MK, unmeasured]: "[Party]-ke [amount] taka dilam"; "[Party]-er kach theke [amount] niyechi"; "[Party] [amount] taka dilo/diyeche"; free reordering of the first; afterthought forms ("dilam Karim ke 500"); item-quantity-price sales ("Rahim ke chini 2 kg 240 taka"); "mot 1000, 600 dilo, baki 400"; verb-only commands ("bill koro", "baki likho"); and a bare "Karim 500" with unknown direction.

### The parsing rules R1 to R13

| Rule | Statement (all [MK] design rules) |
|---|---|
| R1 | Find the verb (usually last, sometimes first) and read its mood: -o/-un command, -lam/-lo past, -yechi/-yeche perfect, -bo future, -na/-ni/-noy negation |
| R2 | Strip name suffixes (-ke, -er, -r, -re, -ere) and match the stem; "Karimer" is "Karim er"; handle spaced and fused forms (kach theke, kacheta theke, kachhe theke) |
| R3 | Bind postpositions: ke = recipient or object; er kach theke / theke = source; diye = channel or intermediary; e / te = location or channel ("bkash e"); er = "of" (Rahim er baki is Rahim's due) |
| R4 | Number chunk = digits or words + optional multiplier (shoto, hajar, lakh) + optional currency (taka, tk, ৳) + optional unit; currency follows the number in Bangla, but "tk 500" appears in typed text |
| R5 | Accept Latin digits, Bangla digits ০–৯ and mixed forms ("৫00"); convert Bangla digits first |
| R6 | Number words to value: "dui hajar" 2000; "dui hajar pach sho" 2500; "sare tin hajar" 3500; "sare tin sho" 350; "deRh hajar" (দেড় হাজার) 1500; "aRai hajar" (আড়াই হাজার) 2500; "paune dui hajar" 1750; "soya sho" 125; "ek lakh" 100000; "pachsho" 500. Sare/soya/paune/deRh/aRai modify the following unit; dozen and hali are multipliers |
| R7 | Collisions to validate: নয় (nine) versus নয় (is not); পাঁচ, পাঁচশ, পঞ্চাশ, পনেরো; ত্রিশ versus তিনশ; সাত, সাতাশ, সাতশ; দুই, দুই শ, দুই হাজার; "ek" as 1 or "a". Speech-to-text drops "sho/hajar", so compare with the party's usual range and the stated total |
| R8 | Latin shorthand "2k", "5/-", "500/=", "tk", "tka", "৳" is acceptable but read back in words |
| R9 | Units: ta, ti, khana, pis, pcs, jon, dojon, hali, kg, gm, ser, pouya, litre, packet, carton, bosta, joda; ask when a quantity has no unit and the item has more than one |
| R10 | Checksum for baki sentences: total minus paid equals baki; if not, do not save and ask which number is wrong |
| R11 | Absolute versus delta: "dam 20 komao" = reduce by 20; "dam 20 koro / 20 e dao" = set to 20; "120 theke 100 koro" = change 120 to 100 (theke is the from-value, not a party); "5 barao" = +5; "stock 5 koro" = set to 5 |
| R12 | Date words: kal = yesterday or tomorrow, porshu = day after tomorrow or day before yesterday; resolve by tense ("kal dilam" yesterday, "kal dibo" tomorrow); the earlier report gives the fuller kal rule |
| R13 | Several numbers in one sentence (amount, quantity, price): require a card that labels each |

The voice-to-text risk list [MK] adds names split or merged ("Abdul Karim" versus "Karim"), same-sound names (Rahim/Rohim, Karim/Korim, Salam/Salim), nicknames and shop names ("Mia bhai", "dokaner Karim"), titles (bhai, apa, khala, chacha, mama), a script mismatch with stored names (Bangla versus Latin), code-mixed numerals ("five hundred taka", "পাঁচ hundred"), misheard dilam/dilo, dropped sho/hajar, and two customers with the same name. Names are matched to saved parties by stem, candidates are shown with a distinguishing hint (area, last transaction), and the assistant never auto-picks when two score close. Normalise Unicode (NFC, nukta forms ড়/ঢ়, ্য/য়) before matching, and expect speech-to-text spelling variants (বাকি/বাকী, ফেরত/ফেরৎ, বায়না/বায়নাহ). Behaviour of Bangla speech recognition on verb endings (-lam/-lo) is untested; the notes found no source.

### Negation, correction and confirmation words

Cited grammar: না follows the finite verb ("kaj kori na"), -নি negates perfect forms ("korini"), নেই negates existence, and the copula negation is নই/নও/নন/নয় ([Wikipedia: Bengali grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_grammar)); "nai" is an ordinary colloquial negation ("kono bhangti nai") ([Wikibooks Bengali/Shopping](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Bengali/Shopping)). Every correction and cancel phrase below is [MK], and the researcher found no source for any of them.

| Word or phrase | Reading |
|---|---|
| না / na, na na | no or not; also the coaxing "dao na" (please give) |
| noy, "ei ta noy", "ota noy" | not this one (choosing between candidates) |
| ঠিক না / thik noy / thik hoyni | not right; says it is wrong, not what to change |
| ভুল / bhul hoyeche / vul | a mistake: edit the pending or last entry, do not delete |
| আবার / abar | again: either a repeat (duplicate danger) or "redo it" (correction); ask |
| আগেরটা / শেষটা বাতিল / ager ta batil | cancel the previous or last entry; needs identification and a card |
| থাক / thak | leave it, never mind (abort pending), but also "leave the old value" |
| বাদ দাও / bad dao | drop that line or item |
| লাগবে না / dorkar nai | not needed; only a negation |

Confirmation words [MK]: হ্যাঁ / হ্যা / han / ha / hu / hum, ঠিক আছে / ঠিকাছে, ওকে / ok, করো / করে দাও / করে ফেলো, হবে, আছে, জি, সেভ করো. The risks the notes name are that "thik ache" is often a reaction ("fine, I see") rather than consent to save, "hobe" can mean "that will do" or "it will happen", "hu/hum" can be filler, "ha" and "na" are one letter apart on a phone keyboard, and a spoken "na" after a negative question may confirm the negative or deny the question (uncertain).

## Confirm-before-act: tiers, the card, voice, idempotency and hard stops

The action researcher cites no design standard, so the design below is reasoning from the language findings and from the project's own colour and recovery rules (green money, red owed or finished, blue go, icon beside colour; status, undo, next step and retry required). The reason is real: customers told TBS News that shop owners can write more than the actual amount, and an instant SMS of price and total due reduced disputes for one retailer's Tk2,913 month-end balance ([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/feature/panorama/tallykhata-digitising-culture-buying-and-selling-credit-154465)). Competitor apps already show payable, receivable and cash-at-hand totals and balance per customer, which are the numbers a card should echo ([TallyKhata blog](https://www.tallykhata.com/7-reasons-why-tallykhata-is-the-perfect-bookkeeping-payment-solution-for-shops-and-small-businesses/)). A Business Post piece describes halkhata as a notebook record system kept by small shopkeepers, with poor baki management called a common cause of shop failure (snippet only, [Business Post](https://businesspostbd.com/opinion-todays-paper/lost-tradition-of-halkhata-2023-04-14)).

### Risk tiers

| Tier | Actions | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| A: money or balance changes | payment in or out, joma, dhar dilam/nilam, baki likho, shodh, advance or bayna, supplier payment, refund (ferot), bill with credit, waiver, any cancel, delete or undo of a money record | full confirmation card; no voice-only commit; reversal by counter-entry |
| B: stock changes | mal eseche, mal gelo, damage, stock count (SET), exchange | full card; stock count and damage especially; show item, qty, unit, before to after |
| C: master data | dam bodlao (delta versus set), naam bodlao, add item or customer | card with old to new; price changes show the delta explicitly |
| D: read-only | koto baki, stock koto, hishab dekhao | no card |

The notes recommend that every write goes through a card in version one, and that skipping the card (with Undo) is a product decision for the owner, to be made only after measurement.

### What the card must show

The card has eight parts. First, the verb in plain Bangla past tense with the subject made explicit and the original words quoted: "আপনি করিমকে ৫০০ টাকা দিয়েছেন" (you gave Karim 500 taka) versus "করিম আপনাকে ৫০০ টাকা দিয়েছে" (Karim gave you 500 taka). Second, a party chip with its role (customer, supplier, staff) and a disambiguator when more than one candidate matches (phone ending, last transaction). Third, the amount in large digits and in words (৫০০ - পাঁচশো টাকা), with Bangladeshi lakh-style separators (1,00,000). Fourth, a direction arrow and words ("টাকা ঢুকলো / টাকা বের হলো") with the colour code and an icon so colour is not the only cue. Fifth, an effect line: "করিমের কাছে পাওনা: ১২০০ → ১৭০০", with who owes whom spelled out and a warning if the balance crosses zero. Sixth, the date (default today, shown as "আজ") and the channel (cash, bKash, Nagad). Seventh, for bills and returns the line items with quantity, unit and price, and for baki sentences the total, paid and baki with the equation checked. Eighth, buttons: Save, Change (opens the pending fields) and Cancel, with an optional "wrong person?" swap. The notes also want the verb ending shown in bold when the sentence contained dilam, dilo, nilam or nilo, so the user sees the cue the system used, and two parsed candidates shown when the model is torn ("আপনি দিলেন / করিম দিলো?"). The Bangla button wording ("Save (সেভ করুন)", "Change (বদলান)", "Cancel (বাতিল)") is the notes' proposal, and the notes caution that the card should not copy mobile-financial-service wording (bKash Cash Out, Send Money) until verified and that no evidence says whether users prefer "Save" or "ঠিক আছে".

### Voice confirmation

Never ask a negative question ("save na korbo?"). Accept a spoken confirmation only if it is on a whitelist (hya, ha, thik ache, ok, koro, kore dao, save koro) and only while the card is on screen and the same card was read back; any other utterance changes or cancels the card, and "na", "noy", "thik na", "bhul", "thak", "bad dao" and "ageta" are treated as stop signals. For voice-sourced amounts, read the amount back in words, and for amounts of 1,000 or more require a second look (a tap on the figure to edit). Keep the original text for audit, show speech-recognition confidence only as a hint and never as a gate. A correction applies to the pending card first ("500 na, 5000" replaces the amount and re-shows); for a saved record, show the saved entry with Edit and Undo and require a new card for the reversal, which posts a counter-entry and never silently deletes. "Cancel the last one" must show which one (party, amount, time) before it executes. A visible Undo for about 10 to 30 seconds after saving is the notes' suggestion.

### Idempotency

Create a client-side idempotency key when the card is made, and have the save endpoint accept it once, so a double tap or retry returns the first result. Add a content fingerprint (party, amount, direction, kind, date within a few minutes); if seen, show "এটা আগেই সেভ হয়েছে (৩ মিনিট আগে) - আবার সেভ করবেন?" and do not silently drop legitimate repeats, since two 500s from the same customer happen. Treat "abar" or a repeated sentence in the voice buffer as a duplicate candidate, not a new transaction. Offline or queued saves keep the same key and show a pending state until acknowledged, with the key checked first on retry. Reversals are new linked entries; Undo applies only to the exact record shown. The idempotency window sizes are guesses and the notes found no data on how often shopkeepers repeat a sentence into voice input.

### Hard stops that become questions

The assistant asks a question instead of showing a card when: the party, the amount or the direction is missing, or the sentence is verb-only ("joma koro", "Karim 500"); two parties or two amounts appear in one sentence; the party role conflicts with the verb ("supplier ke dhar dilam"); the amount is outside the party's normal range (the notes suggest 10 times the median or more) or a number's reading collides (noy/nine, tirish/tinsho); total minus paid is not baki; tense is future or past mismatched (dibo and pabo offer "set reminder" or "record as due", not a payment); the sentence contains a negation or hedge (na, noy, thak, pore, dekhi) or is a question; or it is a correction to an earlier saved record. This is the point where the recommended design most clearly overrides convenience, since the notes accept the cost of an extra question to avoid a flipped balance.

## How to use this in the assistant: a staged order

The ordering below is a design recommendation derived from the notes' risk tiers and guard rules, not a finding. It builds only what the evidence says is low-risk first.

**Stage 0, normalise and classify (safe now).** Normalise Unicode, convert Bangla digits, strip address terms, greetings and politeness fillers, and split verb, noun, name and number chunks as R1 to R5 describe. Classify into the six types using the cue sets above, with the guard rule that advice-shaped, negated, hedged or question-shaped sentences never reach a write path. Resolve kal and baki by the earlier report's defaults.

**Stage 1, the command rule and bare nouns (safe now, read-only).** Stem the verb to the show, tell, list, search, count, print families, treat tui, tumi, apni and polite-future forms as one intent, accept the "do it for me" and wish forms, and treat a lone data noun (with optional time, name and scope words) as "show current X". Check collisions (dao, tel, chal, tulo, bol, list, kor, pathao) against the shop's own catalogue before treating a token as a verb. Answer with data. Reply in apni forms.

**Stage 2, advice detection and decline (safe now, read-only).** Detect the advice frames (first-person future + ki / na ki, uchit, bhalo / labh / thik hobe, poramorsho / suggestion / advice den, A naki B, konta). Return the safe data part from the answer table with the owner-set rule stated aloud (reorder level, "no sale in N days", credit limit, minimum margin), name what the data cannot say, and hand the decision back. Decline on the rows marked decline (forecasts, wage and hiring, loans and expansion, legal and tax and religious questions, character judgements, and any medicine or dosage question for a pharmacy). Because the owner-set rules do not exist until the owner sets them, the first version can show the raw numbers without a threshold.

**Stage 3, "why" questions (safe after stage 1 data works).** Answer with period-over-period differences visible in the owner's records; list outside causes as unknown; ask a metric question for "best customer".

**Stage 4, send, share and print (needs a confirm step first).** Printing and exporting on the device stays read-only. Anything that sends text to another person (reminders, "tagada", WhatsApp or SMS to a customer) leaves the phone; the advice notes say draft text only if asked and send only on confirmation.

**Stage 5, the confirm flow, then actions.** Build the card, the stop-word voice confirmation, the idempotency key and the hard-stop questions before enabling any write. Then enable writes. The notes do not rank individual actions, but their tiers suggest starting with tier C (add item or customer, rename), then tier B (stock in), and leaving tier A money events and any set-versus-change edit (price, stock count) until the direction and delta rules have been validated with staff. All of the "set versus change" rules (R11) apply to both stock and price.

**Stage 6, corrections and undo.** Pending-card corrections come first (they are the cheapest). Cancelling or reversing a saved record comes last, because it is itself a tier A action that needs identification and a counter-entry.

Throughout, the three ambiguities that decide correctness need the same explicit handling the earlier report set: direction of money (never from the noun alone), baki (remainder, due or change), and kal (yesterday or tomorrow, with nogod, cash versus Nagad, handled by the earlier table).

## Validation with real Bangladeshi shop staff

Nothing in this report has been tested on real users, so a validation round must precede any hard-coded rule. The notes' own gaps list is the checklist. The earlier report suggested 3 to 5 shopkeepers per market, 50 to 100 real utterances per business type, plus checking medicine data and the Dakshina lexicon; the same sample sizes are a reasonable start here, though the notes do not set a size for this topic.

The first task is to collect real messages. Ask staff to say and type how they would tell a phone helper to show, search, send, record, correct and cancel things, and also what they would ask when they need a decision. Capture both voice dictation and typing, in Bangla script and in Banglish, with the tui, tumi or apni level they naturally use (the tumi-default assumption is [MK]). Measure which wrappers, address terms and fillers appear, and the real frequency of bare-noun and verb-less forms.

The second task is to test the high-risk minimal pairs on both spoken and typed input: dilam versus dilo, nilam versus nilo, with and without a subject, and with ke, -re, theke and "er kach theke"; whether the -re form really appears in BD Banglish; whether voice-to-text keeps the ending. Test baki, dhar, joma, shodh and ferot in complete sentences and have staff say which direction each means, and check "pabo/dibo" used as a balance statement, "mal tola" (in or out), "mal tulechi", "ulte dao" as undo, and the meaning of "joma dao" and "joma nao". Test number reading with the collisions (noy versus nine, pachsho, tirish versus tinsho, deRh, aRai, sare, paune), code-mixed numerals, and amounts over 99.

The third task is to test the advice and explanation side. Confirm which advice frames owners really use (kormu/dimu forms by region, "korbo ki" versus "ki korbo", "labh" versus "lobh"), whether "advice" and "suggestion" are the usual English loans, and whether owners accept a reply that only shows numbers. Rank the topics by frequency (the notes could not), and check trade-specific wording for pharmacy, mobile-recharge or financial-service agents and tailors. Test whether "kon customer shobcheye bhalo" means spend or payment behaviour.

The fourth task is to test the confirm design. Check whether "thik ache", "hobe", "hu" and a short "ha" are read as consent, how people answer a negative question with na/hya, whether the stop signals are understood, whether Save or ঠিক আছে works better as a button, whether the 10 to 30 second Undo window and the 10-times-median amount check are sensible, and how often staff repeat a sentence. Capture the real Bangla labels of TallyKhata, Hishabee, Dokani and bKash from screenshots of the installed apps, since the Play Store and web pages gave titles only, and check the label table and the BD-versus-WB spellings (-re versus -ke, দিব versus দেব) in those screenshots.

For evaluation, split test data by template, not at random, because the dataset finding is that a naive split inflates accuracy to about 99.9% by memorising templates. Report precision on the write path separately from recall on the read path, since a missed command costs a retry and a wrongly parsed payment costs a balance.

## Conclusion

The notes show that the safe boundary in this domain is a grammatical one, and that the boundary can be drawn before the vocabulary is validated. A sentence that asks (a question word, "ki", a hedge, a negation, a future) and a sentence that reports (a past give or take verb with a party and an amount) share almost no cues, so the assistant can refuse to write on any asking-shaped sentence and still be useful, because every asking-shaped sentence has a read-only answer. Direction of money is the opposite kind of problem: it is carried by a single vowel, a dropped subject and a postposition that the speech-to-text layer may lose, which is why a card that restates the sentence with an explicit subject and an arrow is the right unit of safety, not a better classifier.

Two things remain open. The vocabulary is model knowledge throughout; the one thing the verified sources add is grammar (the three imperative levels, the give and take paradigms, the case and negation markers, the number words), and none of it is shop-specific. And several conflicts were left unresolved on purpose: -re versus -ke, দিব versus দেব, the tumi default, the meaning of "mal tola" and "ulte dao", whether "na" after a negative question confirms or denies, and the exact Bangla labels in the competing apps. The first real shopkeeper utterances will decide which of these matter, so the staged order starts with the read-only parts that need no resolution.
